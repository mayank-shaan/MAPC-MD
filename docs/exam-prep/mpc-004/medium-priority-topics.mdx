## 📖 Topic Summaries

### 1. Cooperation and Competition
**Most likely as**: Section B (6-mark) or Section C (short note)

Questions ask to define cooperation, differentiate cooperation from competition, discuss types of cooperation, or describe cooperation and competition as processes of social interaction.

**Key concepts**: Prisoner's dilemma, social exchange theory, superordinate goals (Sherif's Robbers Cave), interdependence theory, game theory basics.

### 2. Self-Perception
**Most likely as**: Section B (6-mark)

Bem's self-perception theory states that people infer their own attitudes by observing their own behaviour — particularly when internal cues are weak. Questions ask to explain the theory or describe how self-perception differs from cognitive dissonance theory.

**Key concepts**: Bem (1972), over-justification effect, intrinsic vs extrinsic motivation, contrast with Festinger's dissonance theory.

### 3. Social Identity
**Most likely as**: Section B (6-mark) or Section C (short note)

Questions ask about social identity theory (Tajfel and Turner), in-group favouritism, out-group derogation, and sometimes the role of fashion in everyday social identity.

**Key concepts**: Social categorisation → Social identification → Social comparison, minimal group paradigm, self-esteem and group membership.

### 4. Nature, Scope and History of Social Psychology
**Most likely as**: Section A (10-mark) or Section B (6-mark) — appears more in recent papers

Questions ask to define social psychology, trace its historical development, or discuss its relationship with related disciplines (sociology, anthropology, personality psychology).

**Key concepts**: Triplett's (1898) cycling experiment, McDougall and Ross (1908), Floyd Allport's individual focus, Lewin's field theory, crisis of confidence in the 1970s, current trends (cross-cultural, applied, cognitive social psychology).
