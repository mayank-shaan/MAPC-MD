## Key Question: History and Development of Social Psychology / Nature and Scope (Section A)

### Answer (~450 words):

Social psychology is the scientific study of how people's thoughts, feelings, and behaviours are influenced by the actual, imagined, or implied presence of others (Allport, 1954). It occupies the intersection of individual psychology and sociology, focusing on the social determinants of individual behaviour.

**Nature of Social Psychology:**
Social psychology is scientific (uses empirical methods), focuses on the individual in social context (not groups as abstract entities), and studies current social influences rather than historical development (distinguishing it from sociology and developmental psychology). It emphasises the power of social situations over internal dispositions.

**Historical Development:**

*Pre-scientific Phase (before 1900)*: Social philosophical speculation about human nature (Plato, Aristotle, Hobbes, Rousseau) laid conceptual groundwork but lacked empirical methodology.

*Founding Phase (1897–1930)*: Norman Triplett (1898) conducted the first social psychology experiment — cyclists pedalled faster when racing against others than alone, demonstrating social facilitation. William McDougall (1908) published "An Introduction to Social Psychology" emphasising instincts; E.A. Ross (1908) published a sociologically oriented "Social Psychology." These twin births established the field.

*Behaviourist Dominance (1930–1950)*: Watson's behaviourism focused social psychology on observable, measurable behaviour. Attitudes became a central focus — Gordon Allport (1935) defined attitude as "the most distinctive and indispensable concept in American social psychology."

*Gestalt and Lewin's Influence (1940–1960)*: Kurt Lewin emigrated from Germany and introduced Gestalt principles to social psychology. His field theory (B = f(P, E)) — behaviour is a function of the person and their psychological environment — launched group dynamics, action research, and experimental social psychology as we know it.

*Era of Great Experiments (1950–1970)*: Landmark studies by Asch (conformity), Milgram (obedience), Festinger (cognitive dissonance), and Sherif (realistic conflict) established the canon of social psychology.

*Crisis of Confidence (1970s)*: Concerns about generalisability, cross-cultural validity, and the ethics of deception led to methodological and ethical reforms.

*Contemporary Period (1980–present)*: Social cognition (how people think about social information), cultural psychology (cross-cultural variation in social behaviour), evolutionary social psychology, applied social psychology, and big-data approaches characterise the current field.

**Scope and Applications:**
Social psychology studies attitudes, social influence, group behaviour, aggression, pro-social behaviour, stereotyping, attribution, and interpersonal attraction. Applications include health psychology (behaviour change), legal psychology (jury decision-making), organisational behaviour (leadership, motivation), and environmental psychology (conservation behaviour).

**Related Disciplines:**
Unlike sociology (which studies social structures and institutions), social psychology focuses on the individual. Unlike personality psychology (which emphasises stable internal traits), social psychology emphasises situational determinants. Cultural psychology extends social psychological principles across cultural contexts.
