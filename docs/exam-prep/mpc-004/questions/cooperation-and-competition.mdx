## Key Question: Cooperation and Competition as Processes of Social Interaction

### Answer (Section A — ~450 words):

Cooperation and competition are fundamental processes through which individuals and groups interact to achieve goals. They represent opposite ends of a social interdependence continuum.

**Cooperation** is a process in which two or more individuals or groups work together toward a shared goal, combining efforts, resources, and skills for mutual benefit. It involves positive interdependence — each member's success depends on and facilitates others' success.

**Competition** is a process in which individuals or groups work against each other to achieve a goal that not all can attain. It involves negative interdependence — one party's success comes at another's expense.

**Types of Cooperation (Anderson):**
*Spontaneous cooperation*: Arises naturally without planning — helping a stranger or sharing resources in an emergency.
*Traditional cooperation*: Governed by long-standing customs and cultural norms — communal farming, neighbourhood mutual aid.
*Directed cooperation*: Organised by a leader or institution toward specified goals — corporate teamwork, organised sports.
*Contractual cooperation*: Governed by explicit agreements — business partnerships, treaties.

**Differentiation: Cooperation vs Competition**

| Dimension | Cooperation | Competition |
|-----------|-------------|-------------|
| Goal structure | Shared, mutually beneficial | Individual, mutually exclusive |
| Interdependence | Positive (linked success) | Negative (one wins, one loses) |
| Communication | Open, honest | Guarded, strategic |
| Outcome | Win-win | Win-lose (zero-sum) |
| Relationship effect | Builds trust and cohesion | May damage relationships |

**Cooperation and Competition in Social Interaction:**
Deutsch (1949) demonstrated that cooperatively structured groups showed higher productivity, better coordination, and more friendly interaction than competitively structured groups. However, competition can enhance individual motivation and performance in simple, well-defined tasks.

**Prisoner's Dilemma:**
The Prisoner's Dilemma game captures the tension between individual rationality (defect/compete) and collective rationality (cooperate). Two prisoners, unable to communicate, must independently choose to cooperate (stay silent) or defect (betray). If both cooperate, both receive a light sentence. If one defects and the other cooperates, the defector goes free and the cooperator receives the heaviest sentence. If both defect, both receive a moderate sentence. Individual rationality drives both to defect, producing a suboptimal outcome for both — illustrating why competition often leads to collectively inferior outcomes compared to cooperation.

**Factors Promoting Cooperation:**
Communication, trust, long-term relationship orientation, use of superordinate goals, equal power, and prior cooperative experience all increase cooperation.

In conclusion, cooperation and competition are not fixed opposites but situationally determined processes shaped by goal structures, interdependence, communication, and cultural context.
