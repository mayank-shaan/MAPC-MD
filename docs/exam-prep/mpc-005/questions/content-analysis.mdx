## 🔥 Key Question: Content Analysis (Short Note — Section C / Section B)

### Short Note Answer (~120 words):

Content analysis is a systematic, replicable method for analysing the content of communication — text, images, audio, or video. It identifies, codes, and categorises patterns or themes in the data. Two forms exist: **manifest content analysis** (counts observable, explicit features — e.g., frequency of a word) and **latent content analysis** (interprets underlying meanings and themes). Steps include: (a) select and define the unit of analysis; (b) develop a coding scheme; (c) train coders; (d) code data and check inter-rater reliability; (e) analyse and interpret frequencies or themes. Content analysis is more systematic and quantifiable than discourse analysis, but lacks the latter's focus on how language constructs social realities. It is widely used in media analysis, policy research, and clinical psychology.

## 📝 Section B Answer — Content Analysis in Detail (~250 words):

**Definition:**
Content analysis is defined by Berelson (1952) as "a research technique for the objective, systematic, and quantitative description of the manifest content of communication." More recently, it has expanded to include qualitative, interpretive approaches that examine latent meaning.

**Types:**
- *Manifest content analysis*: Analyses surface-level, visible features (e.g., counting how many times "aggression" appears in a text). Objective and replicable but may miss deeper meaning.
- *Latent content analysis*: Interprets the underlying themes, intentions, or symbolic meanings. More interpretive; requires theoretical framework.
- *Quantitative content analysis*: Produces frequencies, proportions, and statistical comparisons.
- *Qualitative content analysis*: Produces themes and categories grounded in data; closer to thematic analysis.

**Steps:**
1. *Define research question and unit of analysis*: Units can be words, sentences, paragraphs, themes, or entire documents.
2. *Select and sample material*: Choose texts to be analysed; systematic sampling ensures representativeness.
3. *Develop coding scheme (codebook)*: Define categories or codes; each should be mutually exclusive and exhaustive.
4. *Train coders*: Multiple coders increase reliability; pilot coding is done to refine categories.
5. *Code the data*: Systematically apply codes to all units of analysis.
6. *Check inter-rater reliability*: Calculate agreement (Cohen's kappa or percentage agreement).
7. *Analyse and interpret*: Tally frequencies or develop thematic maps; relate to research question.
8. *Report findings*: Present with examples of coded material.

**Content Analysis vs Discourse Analysis:**

| Dimension | Content Analysis | Discourse Analysis |
|-----------|-----------------|-------------------|
| Focus | What is said; frequency | How language constructs meaning |
| Epistemology | Positivist | Constructionist |
| Output | Quantitative categories or themes | Thick interpretation of discourse practices |
| Reliability | Inter-rater reliability | Credibility through reflexivity |
