## 🔥 Key Question: Field Experiment — Steps, Strengths & Weaknesses (Section A/B)

### Answer (~400 words):

**Definition:**
A field experiment is a research method in which the researcher manipulates an independent variable in a natural, real-world setting rather than in a controlled laboratory. Unlike correlational or quasi-experimental research, the researcher actively controls the IV. Unlike laboratory experiments, participants are typically unaware they are in a study, which enhances ecological validity.

Classic example: Darley and Latané's (1968) bystander intervention studies, Milgram's obedience studies (partially), and traffic-safety interventions in real communities.

**Characteristics:**
1. IV is actively manipulated by the researcher.
2. Takes place in a natural setting (field).
3. Participants are usually unaware of their participation.
4. Random assignment may or may not be possible.
5. Less control over extraneous variables than lab experiments.

**Steps in Conducting a Field Experiment:**

1. *Identify research question and formulate hypothesis*: Frame a clear, testable hypothesis about real-world behaviour.
2. *Select the field setting*: Choose a natural environment where the behaviour of interest occurs (school, hospital, public space).
3. *Operationalise variables*: Define how the IV will be manipulated and how the DV will be measured in the field.
4. *Plan for randomisation*: If possible, randomly assign units (individuals, groups, classrooms) to conditions.
5. *Pilot test procedures*: Test feasibility of manipulation and measurement in the field setting.
6. *Implement the intervention*: Introduce the IV manipulation without alerting participants.
7. *Collect data*: Observe or measure DV using unobtrusive measures or covert observation.
8. *Control for extraneous variables*: Attempt to hold constant any field conditions that might confound results.
9. *Analyse and interpret data*: Use appropriate statistical analysis; account for clustering if groups are the unit of assignment.
10. *Debrief (if ethically required)*: Inform participants after data collection.

**Strengths:**

| Strength | Explanation |
|----------|-------------|
| Ecological validity | Behaviour occurs in natural context |
| Reduced demand characteristics | Participants unaware, so less likely to alter behaviour |
| External validity | Findings generalise to real-world settings |
| Practical applicability | Tests interventions in realistic conditions |

**Weaknesses:**

| Weakness | Explanation |
|----------|-------------|
| Poor control | Cannot eliminate all extraneous variables |
| Ethical issues | Studying people without informed consent raises ethical concerns |
| Replication difficulty | Natural settings vary; hard to replicate exactly |
| Measurement challenges | DV measurement in natural settings is complex |
| Possible attrition | Participants may drop out or move settings |

**Lab vs Field Experiment:**

| Dimension | Laboratory Experiment | Field Experiment |
|-----------|-----------------------|-----------------|
| Setting | Controlled lab | Natural environment |
| IV control | High | Moderate |
| Random assignment | Usually yes | Sometimes yes |
| Ecological validity | Low | High |
| Demand characteristics | Higher | Lower |
| Ethical scrutiny | Moderate | Higher (deception in natural settings) |
