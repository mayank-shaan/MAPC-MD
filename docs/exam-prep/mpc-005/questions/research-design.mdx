## 🔥 Key Question: Definition, Objectives and Types of Research Design (Section A)

### Answer (~400 words):

**Definition:**
A research design is the overall plan or blueprint for conducting a study. It specifies the procedures for collecting, organising, and analysing data to answer the research question. Kerlinger (1986) defines it as the plan, structure, and strategy of investigation conceived to obtain answers to research questions and to control variance. It is the roadmap that guides every subsequent decision in the research process.

**Objectives/Functions of Research Design:**

1. *To answer research questions*: The design specifies what data to collect and how, ensuring the study is capable of answering the research question.

2. *To control variance*: A good design maximises explained variance (effect of IV), controls extraneous variance, and minimises error variance.
   - *Maximise systematic variance*: Ensure the IV conditions are sufficiently different to produce detectable effects.
   - *Control extraneous variance*: Use randomisation, matching, or statistical control to prevent confounding.
   - *Minimise error variance*: Standardise procedures, use reliable measures.

3. *To ensure internal validity*: Ensures that observed effects on the DV are genuinely caused by the IV, not by confounding variables.

4. *To facilitate generalisation (external validity)*: Designs that use representative samples and realistic settings allow findings to be generalised.

5. *To guide data collection and analysis*: Specifies what instruments will be used and what statistical analyses are appropriate.

**Types of Research Design:**

| Type | Key Feature | Example |
|------|-------------|---------|
| Experimental | Random assignment, IV manipulation | RCT for drug efficacy |
| Quasi-experimental | IV manipulation, no random assignment | Pre-post design in schools |
| Correlational | No manipulation, measures association | Anxiety–performance correlation |
| Survey/Descriptive | Describes phenomena, no manipulation | Opinion polls |
| Qualitative | Non-numerical, interpretive | Grounded theory study |
| Ex-Post Facto | IV occurred in past, no manipulation | Effects of childhood trauma |
| Single-subject | Intensive study of one unit | ABA reversal design |

**Criteria of a Good Research Design:**
1. *Adequacy*: The design addresses the research question directly.
2. *Economy*: Uses minimum resources to achieve sufficient power.
3. *Reliability*: Produces consistent results if repeated.
4. *Validity*: Both internal (attributing effects correctly) and external (generalising).
5. *Flexibility*: Allows modification if needed without compromising integrity.
