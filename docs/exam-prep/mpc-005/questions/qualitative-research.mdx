## Key Question: Qualitative vs Quantitative Research and Types of Qualitative Research

### Answer (Section A — ~450 words):

Qualitative research is an inquiry approach that seeks to understand phenomena from the perspective of participants, emphasising meaning, context, and process rather than measurement, prediction, and generalisation. It is constructivist in orientation — assuming that reality is socially constructed and multiple.

**Qualitative vs Quantitative Research:**

| Dimension | Qualitative | Quantitative |
|-----------|------------|--------------|
| Ontology | Multiple realities; subjective | Single, objective reality |
| Epistemology | Constructivist/interpretive | Positivist/post-positivist |
| Purpose | Understand meaning, context, process | Measure, predict, generalise |
| Data | Words, images, observations | Numbers, statistics |
| Sampling | Purposive, theoretical, snowball | Random, representative |
| Sample size | Small | Large |
| Analysis | Thematic, interpretive, narrative | Statistical |
| Generalisation | Transferability (contextual) | Statistical generalisation |
| Strengths | Depth, contextual richness, discovery | Precision, replicability, generalisation |

**Types of Qualitative Research:**

*1. Phenomenology*
Studies the lived experience of individuals with a particular phenomenon. Aims to describe the "essence" of experience as it appears to consciousness. Associated with Husserl (transcendental phenomenology) and Heidegger (hermeneutic phenomenology). Method: in-depth interviews; bracket pre-existing assumptions (epoché); extract meanings and themes.

*2. Ethnography*
Involves prolonged immersion in a cultural setting to understand participants' worldview from within. Uses participant observation, field notes, and interviews. Produces "thick description" (Geertz). See Ethnography topic page for full detail.

*3. Grounded Theory*
Inductively develops theory grounded in data through theoretical sampling and constant comparison. Uses open, axial, and selective coding. See Grounded Theory topic page for full detail.

*4. Case Study*
In-depth investigation of a single case (individual, organisation, event, or programme). Uses multiple data sources (interviews, documents, observation). Aims for comprehensive understanding of a complex phenomenon in context.

*5. Narrative Inquiry*
Studies experience through stories told by participants. Analyses how people construct meaning through narrative. Associated with Clandinin and Connelly.

*6. Action Research*
Collaborative, cyclical research aimed at solving a practical problem while generating knowledge. Common in educational and organisational settings.

**Relevance of Qualitative Research in Psychology:**
Many psychological phenomena — meaning, identity, experience, motivation — are not reducible to numbers. Qualitative methods access participants' perspectives directly, generate hypotheses for subsequent quantitative testing, and are particularly valuable in culturally sensitive research, clinical psychology, and health psychology where context is critical.

**Criteria of Quality:**
Lincoln and Guba (1985) proposed: credibility (internal validity equivalent — achieved through prolonged engagement, triangulation, member checking), transferability (external validity — through thick description), dependability (reliability — audit trail), and confirmability (objectivity — reflexivity).
