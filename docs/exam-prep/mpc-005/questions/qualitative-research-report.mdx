## Key Question: Steps in Evaluating and Reporting Qualitative Research Data

### Answer (Section A — ~450 words):

Qualitative data analysis involves making sense of large volumes of textual, visual, or observational data to identify patterns, themes, and meanings. Unlike quantitative analysis (which follows fixed statistical procedures), qualitative analysis is iterative, interpretive, and reflexive.

**Steps in Evaluating/Analysing Qualitative Data:**

*1. Data Management and Preparation*
Transcribe audio/video recordings verbatim. Organise and label all data sources. Create an audit trail documenting all decisions. Use qualitative data analysis software (NVivo, ATLAS.ti) for large datasets.

*2. Familiarisation*
Read and re-read all data to achieve immersion. Note initial impressions and emerging ideas in memos. This stage generates the analyst's first tentative interpretations.

*3. Initial Coding*
Systematically work through data, assigning descriptive codes to segments of data that are relevant to the research question. Codes may be in vivo (using participants' own words) or analyst-constructed. Keep codes close to the data at this stage.

*4. Generating Themes*
Cluster codes into broader themes that capture something important about the data. A theme captures a patterned response or meaning within the dataset. Thematic analysis (Braun and Clarke, 2006) — widely used in psychology — identifies, analyses, and reports themes.

*5. Reviewing and Refining Themes*
Review themes against the coded data (do they fit?) and against the entire dataset (do they represent the dataset?). Merge, split, rename, or discard themes as necessary. Develop a thematic map.

*6. Defining and Naming Themes*
Write a detailed analysis of each theme: what does this theme mean, what does it tell us about the research question? Give each theme a clear, informative name.

*7. Interpreting Findings*
Relate themes to existing theory and literature. Answer the research question. Identify implications, limitations, and directions for future research. Reflexivity — the researcher examines how their own background and assumptions may have shaped interpretation — is essential.

**Contents of a Qualitative Research Report:**

1. *Abstract*: Brief summary of purpose, method, findings, and conclusions.
2. *Introduction*: Background, rationale, and research questions. Review of relevant literature.
3. *Method*: Research design, participants (with purposive sampling rationale), data collection procedures, analysis method, ethical considerations.
4. *Findings/Results*: Themes with supporting data extracts. Extended quotes illustrate each theme.
5. *Discussion*: Interpretation, relation to prior research, theoretical contributions, practical implications.
6. *Conclusion*: Summary of key insights, limitations, and future research directions.
7. *References and Appendices*: Interview schedules, consent forms, codebook.

**Criteria for Evaluating Quality:**
Credibility (prolonged engagement, member checking), transferability (thick description), dependability (audit trail), confirmability (reflexivity statement), and reflexivity.

## 💡 Exam Tips
- Questions often ask for "steps in evaluating data" OR "contents of qualitative research report" — prepare both.
- Thematic analysis (Braun and Clarke) is the most commonly cited specific analytic approach — name it.
- Reflexivity is a marker of sophisticated understanding — always mention it in any qualitative analysis answer.
