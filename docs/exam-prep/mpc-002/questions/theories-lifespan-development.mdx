## 📝 Master Answer (250 words / 6 marks)

### Q. Differentiate between stage theories and contextual approach with reference to adulthood. (6 marks)

**Stage Theories of Adulthood**

Stage theories propose that development proceeds through a fixed, universal sequence of qualitatively distinct stages. Two major stage theorists are:

1. **Erikson's Psychosocial Theory**: Adulthood involves three stages — Intimacy vs Isolation (young adulthood), Generativity vs Stagnation (middle adulthood), and Ego Integrity vs Despair (late adulthood). Each stage involves a predictable psychosocial conflict that must be resolved.

2. **Levinson's Seasons of Life**: Development alternates between stable periods (consolidation of life structure) and transitional periods (questioning and revision). Key adult transitions include: Early Adulthood (17–22), Age 30 transition, Mid-Life Transition (40–45), and Late Adulthood transition (60–65).

**Strengths of Stage Theories**: Provide a clear developmental map; supported by cross-sectional research; useful for counselling and education.

**Contextual Approach (Life Events Approach)**

The contextual approach argues that adult development is not primarily driven by age-based stages but by the **timing, sequencing, and personal meaning of life events** — marriage, career change, bereavement, divorce. Development in this model is less predictable and more individually varied.

**Key Difference**: Stage theories emphasise universal, age-linked sequences; contextual theories emphasise individual differences, social context, and the unique impact of personal life events.

**Modern view**: Most developmental psychologists combine both perspectives — there are some common transitions (normative) but enormous individual variation in how and when they are experienced.
