## 📝 Master Answer (250 words / 6 marks)

### Q. Discuss language development during infancy. (6 marks)

Language development in infancy (0–2 years) is a remarkable process — moving from undifferentiated crying to the production of meaningful words and two-word utterances within 24 months.

**Four Components of Language**

1. **Phonology** — the sound system of language. Infants begin with a broad ability to discriminate all speech sounds and progressively narrow to the sounds of their native language. Babbling (6 months) reflects this phonological development.

2. **Semantics** — the meaning of words and sentences. First words appear around 12 months, typically naming familiar objects or people (mama, dada, cup). By 18 months, a "vocabulary spurt" (fast mapping) occurs — children learn new words rapidly.

3. **Syntax** — the grammatical rules governing sentence structure. Two-word utterances emerge around 18–24 months ("more milk," "daddy go"). These "telegraphic" utterances show early grammatical understanding.

4. **Pragmatics** — the social rules of language use. Infants learn to take turns in conversation, use eye contact to communicate, and adjust communication based on context.

**Stages of Language Development in Infancy**

| Age | Stage |
|-----|-------|
| 0–2 months | Crying and reflexive sounds |
| 2–4 months | Cooing (vowel sounds) |
| 6 months | Babbling (consonant-vowel combinations: "ba-ba-ba") |
| 12 months | First meaningful words |
| 18 months | Vocabulary spurt; 50+ words |
| 24 months | Two-word utterances; ~200–300 words |

**Theories**: Chomsky's nativist theory (Language Acquisition Device) emphasises innate predisposition; social-interactionist theories stress the role of caregivers in scaffolding language development.
