## 📝 Master Answer — Section A (450 words / 10 marks)

### Q. Compare Piaget's and Kohlberg's theories of moral development. (10 marks)

**Introduction**

Moral development refers to the process through which children learn to distinguish right from wrong and develop a sense of ethical responsibility. Two of the most influential theories are those of Jean Piaget and Lawrence Kohlberg, both of whom viewed moral development as proceeding through stages that parallel cognitive development. While Kohlberg built on Piaget's foundational work, the two theories differ in scope, methodology, and depth.

**Piaget's Theory of Moral Development**

Piaget (1932) studied moral development through children's understanding of rules in games and their judgements about moral dilemmas. He identified two major stages:

1. **Heteronomous Morality (Moral Realism)** (up to age 10): Children in this stage view rules as fixed, unchangeable, and imposed by authority (parents, teachers, God). Moral judgement is based on **consequences** (a child who accidentally breaks 10 cups is naughtier than one who deliberately breaks 1). This is called **objective responsibility**.

2. **Autonomous Morality (Moral Relativism)** (10 years onwards): Children begin to understand that rules are social agreements that can be changed, and that intentions matter in moral judgement. This shift is driven by cognitive development (moving out of egocentrism) and increased peer interaction.

Piaget also noted a **moral lag** — moral development trails cognitive development by a few years.

**Kohlberg's Theory of Moral Development**

Lawrence Kohlberg (1958, 1969) extended Piaget's theory by presenting children and adults with moral dilemmas (notably the Heinz dilemma) and analysing the reasoning behind their answers. He identified three levels of moral development, each containing two stages:

| Level | Stage | Key Feature |
|-------|-------|-------------|
| **Pre-conventional** (childhood) | Stage 1: Punishment-obedience | Morality = avoiding punishment |
| | Stage 2: Instrumental purpose | Morality = satisfying own needs ("you scratch my back...") |
| **Conventional** (adolescence–adulthood) | Stage 3: Interpersonal conformity | Morality = pleasing others, being a "good boy/girl" |
| | Stage 4: Social order | Morality = following rules, maintaining society |
| **Post-conventional** (rare) | Stage 5: Social contract | Morality = democratic laws that can be changed if unjust |
| | Stage 6: Universal principles | Morality = personal conscience based on universal ethical principles (justice, equality) |

**Comparison of Piaget and Kohlberg**

| Dimension | Piaget | Kohlberg |
|-----------|--------|----------|
| Stages | 2 stages | 3 levels, 6 stages |
| Method | Rule games + moral dilemmas (children) | Heinz dilemma (children + adults) |
| Focus | Intentions vs consequences | Quality of moral reasoning |
| Age range | Childhood | Childhood through adulthood |
| Cultural universality | Limited | Claimed universal; criticised for Western bias |
| Gender | Not addressed | Criticised by Gilligan for male bias |

**Critiques**

Kohlberg's theory was criticised by Carol Gilligan (1982), who argued it reflects a masculine, justice-based morality and ignores the care-based morality more typical in women. Research shows that few individuals reach Stage 5 or 6 consistently.

**Conclusion**

Both Piaget and Kohlberg emphasised that moral development is an active cognitive process shaped by experience and social interaction. Kohlberg's detailed stage model provides a richer framework, but Piaget's foundational insight — that children are moral reasoners, not just rule followers — remains central to understanding moral development.

## 📝 Section B Answer (250 words / 6 marks)

### Q. Explain Piaget's and Kohlberg's ideas on moral development. (6 marks)

**Piaget** studied moral development through children's understanding of rules and moral dilemmas. He identified two stages: (1) **Heteronomous Morality** — children up to age 10 see rules as fixed and base moral judgements on consequences (objective responsibility); (2) **Autonomous Morality** — from age 10, children understand rules as social agreements and judge behaviour by intentions.

**Kohlberg** extended Piaget's work using the Heinz Dilemma. He identified three levels:

- **Pre-conventional** (Stages 1–2): Morality based on punishment avoidance and self-interest
- **Conventional** (Stages 3–4): Morality based on social rules and conformity
- **Post-conventional** (Stages 5–6): Morality based on social contracts and universal ethical principles

**Key difference**: Piaget studied children and focused on the transition from consequence-based to intention-based judgement. Kohlberg studied development across the lifespan and focused on the quality of moral reasoning. Both agree moral development is linked to cognitive growth. Kohlberg was criticised by Gilligan for ignoring a care-based morality more common in women.

## 🧠 Memory Aid

**Kohlberg's Levels — "Pre Con Post"**
- **Pre**-conventional: Self-centred (punishment & reward)
- **Con**ventional: Society-centred (rules & approval)
- **Post**-conventional: Principle-centred (justice & universal ethics)

**Piaget's Stages — "HOAR"**
- **H**eteronomous = consequences-based, rules imposed
- → shifts to →
- **A**utonomous = intentions-based, rules negotiated
- Shift happens around age **10**
