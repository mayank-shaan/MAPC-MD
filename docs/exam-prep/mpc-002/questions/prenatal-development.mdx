## 📝 Master Answer — Stages & Characteristics (450 words / 10 marks)

### Q. Discuss the characteristics and periods of prenatal development. (10 marks)

**Introduction**

Prenatal development refers to the process of biological development from conception to birth — approximately 9 months (40 weeks). During this period, a fertilised egg develops into a fully formed human being through a complex sequence of cellular divisions, differentiation, and growth. Prenatal development is divided into three sequential periods, each characterised by distinct developmental milestones.

**Period 1: Germinal Stage (Weeks 1–2)**

The germinal stage begins at fertilisation when the sperm and ovum unite to form a **zygote** — a single-celled organism containing 46 chromosomes (23 from each parent). Key events include:
- Rapid cell division (cleavage) as the zygote travels down the fallopian tube
- Formation of the **blastocyst** — a hollow ball of cells
- **Implantation** in the uterine wall at approximately Day 8–10

The **trophoblast** (outer layer) becomes the placenta and membranes, while the inner cell mass (embryoblast) becomes the embryo. The most critical event of this stage is successful implantation; approximately 50% of zygotes fail to implant and are lost.

**Period 2: Embryonic Stage (Weeks 3–8)**

The embryonic stage is characterised by the rapid formation of all major organs and body systems. Key features:
- Formation of the **neural tube** (future brain and spinal cord) — Week 3–4
- Heart begins beating — approximately Week 5
- Development of limb buds, eyes, ears, nose — Weeks 4–8
- Formation of the placenta and umbilical cord
- The embryo is approximately 3 cm long by the end of this period

This is the **critical period** for teratogen-induced damage. Teratogens (drugs, alcohol, radiation, infections) can cause severe structural abnormalities during this stage because organ systems are actively forming.

**Period 3: Foetal Stage (Weeks 9–40)**

The foetal stage begins when the embryo is recognisably human. Highlights include:
- Weeks 9–12: External genitals differentiate; foetus can make rudimentary movements; bones begin to ossify
- Weeks 13–24: Rapid growth; foetus develops sucking reflex; responds to sound; viability threshold (around Week 22–24)
- Weeks 25–40: Continued weight gain; brain development accelerates; lungs mature; foetus assumes head-down position for birth

**Conclusion**

Prenatal development is a period of extraordinary transformation — from a single cell to a complex organism with billions of specialised cells. Each stage is sensitive to environmental conditions, emphasising the importance of maternal health, nutrition, and avoidance of teratogens for healthy fetal development.

## 📝 Master Answer — Environmental Influences (450 words / 10 marks)

### Q. Describe the various environmental influences during prenatal development. (10 marks)

**Introduction**

While genetic factors establish the biological blueprint for development, the prenatal environment profoundly shapes how this blueprint is expressed. Any agent that causes developmental abnormalities in the embryo or foetus is called a **teratogen**. The effect of teratogens depends on the timing of exposure (critical period), dosage, genetic vulnerability, and the specific teratogen involved.

**1. Drugs and Medications**

- **Alcohol (FAS)**: Foetal Alcohol Syndrome (FAS) is caused by maternal alcohol consumption. Symptoms include facial malformations, growth retardation, intellectual disability, and behavioural problems. There is no safe level of alcohol during pregnancy.
- **Tobacco**: Smoking increases risk of miscarriage, premature birth, low birth weight, and sudden infant death syndrome (SIDS).
- **Prescription and recreational drugs**: Thalidomide (1950s–60s) caused severe limb defects. Heroin and cocaine exposure leads to neonatal withdrawal symptoms and developmental delays.

**2. Infections**

- **Rubella (German measles)**: First-trimester exposure can cause blindness, deafness, heart defects, and intellectual disability.
- **Toxoplasmosis**: Transmitted through undercooked meat or cat faeces; can cause brain and eye damage.
- **STIs (e.g., syphilis, HIV)**: Can cross the placental barrier and infect the foetus.

**3. Radiation**

X-ray exposure, particularly during the embryonic stage, can cause chromosomal damage and increase cancer risk in the child.

**4. Maternal Nutrition**

Adequate nutrition is essential for fetal growth. Deficiency of:
- **Folic acid**: Causes neural tube defects (spina bifida, anencephaly)
- **Iron**: Causes anaemia in mother, affecting oxygen supply to foetus
- **Iodine**: Can impair brain development

**5. Maternal Age**

- **Teen mothers** (under 18): Risk of premature birth, low birth weight, complications due to immature reproductive system
- **Older mothers** (over 35): Increased risk of chromosomal abnormalities, notably **Down Syndrome** (trisomy 21)

**6. Maternal Stress and Emotional State**

Prolonged stress increases cortisol levels, which can cross the placenta. Chronic stress is linked to premature birth, low birth weight, and behavioural problems in the child.

**7. Rh Factor Incompatibility**

If the mother is Rh-negative and the foetus is Rh-positive, the mother's immune system may produce antibodies that attack foetal red blood cells in subsequent pregnancies, potentially causing jaundice, anaemia, or even foetal death. Prevented through Rh immunoglobulin injection.

**Conclusion**

Prenatal environmental influences highlight the importance of proactive prenatal care — regular medical check-ups, balanced nutrition, avoidance of teratogens, and management of stress. The foetal environment is not passive; it actively shapes the developing organism, demonstrating the fundamental nature-nurture interaction.

## 🧠 Memory Aid

**Three Stages — "GEF"**
- **G**erminal (0–2 weeks): Zygote → implantation
- **E**mbryonic (3–8 weeks): Organ formation — CRITICAL period
- **F**oetal (9–40 weeks): Growth, refinement, maturation

**Teratogens — "DRIMS"**
- **D**rugs (alcohol, tobacco, medications)
- **R**adiation
- **I**nfections (rubella, HIV, toxoplasmosis)
- **M**aternal nutrition (folic acid, iron)
- **S**tress + maternal age

## 💡 Exam Tips

- Questions on prenatal development either ask about **stages/characteristics** OR **environmental influences** — prepare both
- Always name the three stages (germinal, embryonic, foetal) with their time periods at the start of the answer
- For environmental influences: use sub-headings (drugs, infections, nutrition, maternal age, stress, Rh factor)
- Mention **FAS** and **rubella** by name — they are well-known examples that show depth
- Always connect the **critical period** concept when explaining teratogen effects
