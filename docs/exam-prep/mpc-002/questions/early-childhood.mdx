## 📝 Master Answer — Section B (250 words / 6 marks)

### Q. Discuss the characteristics and hazards during early childhood years. (6 marks)

**Early childhood** spans from approximately 2 to 6 years of age. This period is sometimes called the "play age" or "preschool years" and is characterised by expanding physical capabilities, symbolic thinking, and expanding social worlds.

**Key Characteristics:**

1. **Physical growth slows** compared to infancy — children grow steadily, gaining 2–3 kg and approximately 6–8 cm per year. Body proportions become more adult-like.
2. **Motor development**: Large motor skills improve (running, jumping, climbing); fine motor skills develop (drawing, using utensils, dressing). By age 5, most children can ride a bicycle.
3. **Preoperational thinking** (Piaget): Children engage in symbolic and pretend play but show egocentrism (inability to take another's viewpoint), centration, animism, and irreversibility.
4. **Language explosion**: Vocabulary grows from ~200 words at age 2 to ~2,000 words by age 5. Children begin forming complex sentences.
5. **Social development**: Parallel play transitions to cooperative play; children begin forming peer friendships; sex-role socialisation begins.

**Physical Hazards:**
- Accidents and falls (most common cause of childhood injury)
- Infectious diseases
- Nutritional deficiencies
- Dental problems

**Psychological Hazards:**
- Emotional problems: excessive fear, aggression, jealousy
- Failure to develop initiative (Erikson's Stage 3 — Initiative vs Guilt): overprotective or hypercritical parenting can induce excessive guilt, inhibiting the child's natural curiosity and independence
- Speech and language delays
- Effects of harsh discipline

## 📝 Section A Answer — Full Development Overview (450 words)

### Q. Discuss the characteristics of early childhood. Elucidate the physical and psychosocial development in early childhood. (10 marks)

**Introduction**

Early childhood (2–6 years) is a foundational period of development during which children rapidly expand their physical abilities, cognitive capacities, language skills, and social awareness. Often referred to as the "preschool years," this period lays the groundwork for formal schooling and adult competencies.

**Characteristics of Early Childhood**

Early childhood is characterised by: slowing physical growth (compared to infancy); rapid language development; preoperational cognitive thinking (symbolic and imaginative but egocentric); development of self-concept and gender identity; increasingly social behaviour; and significant improvement in fine and gross motor skills.

**Physical Development**

Children in early childhood grow approximately 6–8 cm per year and gain about 2–3 kg annually. The brain continues developing, particularly the prefrontal cortex, enhancing attention and impulse control. Gross motor skills advance rapidly — children can run, climb, hop, and throw by age 3–4. Fine motor skills develop more slowly — drawing, cutting with scissors, and buttoning clothes develop progressively from age 3 to 6.

**Psychosocial Development**

Erikson's third psychosocial stage — **Initiative vs Guilt** — is the central challenge of early childhood. Children become more purposeful and assertive, taking initiative in activities, play, and social interactions. When parents and teachers support this initiative with encouragement and appropriate boundaries, the child develops confidence and purpose. When efforts are consistently criticised or suppressed, excessive guilt develops — inhibiting the child's willingness to try new things and express creativity.

**Play** is the primary vehicle for development in early childhood. Parten (1932) described a progression: solitary play → parallel play → associative play → cooperative play. Play supports cognitive, social, emotional, and language development simultaneously.

**Psychosocial Hazards**

- Excessive punishment or criticism leading to guilt and inhibition
- Sibling rivalry and jealousy
- Inadequate stimulation in learning environments
- Effects of parental conflict or family instability on emotional security

**Conclusion**

Early childhood is a period of extraordinary growth across multiple developmental domains. The quality of nurturing, stimulation, and emotional support provided during this period — by parents, siblings, teachers, and peers — significantly shapes cognitive readiness for school, emotional resilience, and social competence in later childhood and adolescence.
