## 📝 Master Answer — Middle Adulthood Cognitive Changes (250 words / 6 marks)

### Q. Discuss the cognitive changes during middle adulthood. (6 marks)

Cognitive development in middle adulthood (40–60 years) involves both gains and losses — a pattern reflecting the multidirectional nature of lifespan development.

**Fluid vs Crystallised Intelligence (Horn & Cattell)**

- **Fluid intelligence** — the ability to process novel information quickly and engage in abstract reasoning — begins to decline from the mid-20s onwards. In middle adulthood, fluid intelligence is measurably lower than in early adulthood, particularly in speed-dependent tasks.
- **Crystallised intelligence** — accumulated knowledge, vocabulary, and verbal reasoning built through education and experience — continues to increase well into middle and late adulthood. Most middle-aged adults are at the peak of their crystallised intelligence.

**Memory Changes**

- **Working memory** shows some age-related decline in speed and capacity
- **Long-term memory** for meaningful, organised information remains relatively intact
- **Processing speed** slows — middle-aged adults take longer to respond compared to younger adults
- **Episodic memory** (memory for personal events) shows more decline than **semantic memory** (general knowledge)

**Compensatory Mechanisms**

Middle-aged adults compensate for processing speed declines through greater expertise, strategic use of prior knowledge, and more efficient problem-solving approaches. In many professional domains, experienced middle-aged adults outperform younger adults despite slower raw processing speed.

**Conclusion**: Cognitive development in middle adulthood is not simply a story of decline — it is a dynamic balance between fluid ability losses and crystallised knowledge gains, shaped by education, health, and continued engagement.
