## 📝 Short Note — Retirement and Leisure (100 words / 3 marks)

**Retirement** is a major life transition in late adulthood, marking the end of one's occupational role. While retirement offers freedom from work pressure, it also brings challenges: loss of professional identity, reduced social network, financial adjustment, and potential sense of purposelessness. Successful retirement adjustment involves replacing work-based activities with meaningful alternatives — volunteering, grandparenting, hobbies, travel, and community involvement. **Leisure** becomes central to well-being in retirement. Research shows that those who plan for retirement, have good health, and maintain social connections adjust most successfully. Forced or unexpected retirement is associated with more negative outcomes than voluntary retirement.

## 📝 Short Note — Ego Integrity vs Despair (100 words / 3 marks)

**Ego Integrity vs Despair** is Erikson's eighth and final psychosocial stage, occurring in late adulthood. The central task is reflective life review — evaluating whether one's life has been meaningful and worthwhile. **Ego integrity** develops when the person accepts their life — including its imperfections and regrets — with equanimity and a sense that choices were appropriate given the circumstances. It is associated with wisdom and peaceful acceptance of mortality. **Despair** occurs when the person feels life was wasted, mistakes are irreversible, and time is running out. This stage requires having successfully navigated earlier developmental stages, particularly generativity and intimacy.
