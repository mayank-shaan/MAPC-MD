## 📝 Master Answer (250 words / 6 marks)

### Q. Differentiate between self-concept and self-esteem. Describe the signs of negative self-concept in adolescence. (6 marks)

**Self-Concept vs Self-Esteem**

| Dimension | Self-Concept | Self-Esteem |
|-----------|-------------|-------------|
| **Definition** | The cognitive, descriptive view of oneself — "Who am I?" | The evaluative, emotional appraisal of self-worth — "How good am I?" |
| **Nature** | Descriptive (neutral) | Evaluative (positive or negative) |
| **Components** | Academic, physical, social, personal identity | Overall sense of self-worth |
| **Example** | "I am a student, I am tall, I am shy" | "I am a good person / I am worthless" |

Self-concept is broader — it encompasses self-esteem as one evaluative component. Self-esteem specifically refers to how positively or negatively one values oneself.

**Signs of Negative Self-Concept in Adolescence**

Adolescents with a negative self-concept may show:

1. **Persistent self-criticism**: Frequently saying "I am stupid," "I can't do anything right"
2. **Academic avoidance**: Refusing to attempt tasks for fear of failure
3. **Social withdrawal**: Avoiding peers; reluctance to participate in group activities
4. **Sensitivity to criticism**: Over-reacting to mild negative feedback
5. **Comparison with peers**: Constant unfavourable comparison of themselves to others
6. **Low risk-taking**: Refusing new challenges due to anticipated failure
7. **Physical complaints**: Somatic symptoms (headaches, stomach aches) linked to anxiety about performance
8. **Aggressive behaviour**: Sometimes negative self-concept manifests as defensiveness or aggression
9. **Seeking excessive reassurance**: Constantly asking for approval or validation

**Implications**: Negative self-concept in adolescence is associated with depression, anxiety, academic underachievement, and higher risk of substance abuse. Early intervention through counselling and supportive environments is important.
