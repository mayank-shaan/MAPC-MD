## 📝 Short Note — Types of Attachment Patterns (100 words / 3 marks)

**Attachment** is the deep emotional bond between an infant and caregiver, described by Bowlby (1969) as a biological survival mechanism. Ainsworth's Strange Situation experiment (1970) identified four attachment patterns:

1. **Secure attachment**: Distressed when caregiver leaves, quickly soothed on return; associated with sensitive caregiving
2. **Anxious-Ambivalent (Resistant)**: Highly distressed; not easily soothed; conflicting approach-avoidance behaviour toward caregiver
3. **Avoidant**: Shows little distress; ignores caregiver on return; often linked to emotionally unresponsive caregiving
4. **Disorganised**: No consistent strategy; chaotic responses; associated with frightening/abusive caregiving

Secure attachment in infancy predicts healthier emotional and social outcomes in later childhood and adulthood.

## 📝 Section B Answer — Attachment Patterns and Romantic Relationships (250 words)

Bowlby's attachment theory proposes that the early bond between infant and caregiver creates an **internal working model** — a mental template of relationships that guides future social and romantic interactions.

**Ainsworth's Four Attachment Patterns** (see short note above for brief descriptions).

**Attachment in Adulthood**

Hazan and Shaver (1987) applied attachment theory to adult romantic relationships, identifying three parallel styles:
- **Secure** (approximately 55%): Comfortable with intimacy and interdependence; positive view of self and others
- **Anxious-Preoccupied**: Worried about partner's love; jealous; needs constant reassurance
- **Dismissive-Avoidant**: Values independence; uncomfortable with closeness; suppresses attachment needs

Longitudinal research shows that secure attachment in infancy predicts more satisfying, stable romantic relationships in adulthood, though later experiences can modify attachment patterns.
