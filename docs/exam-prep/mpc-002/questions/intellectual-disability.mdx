## 📝 Master Answer (250 words / 6 marks)

### Q. Define mental retardation. Discuss the remedial teaching strategies for children suffering from mental retardation. (6 marks)

**Definition**

**Intellectual Disability** (previously called mental retardation; now the preferred DSM-5 term) is a neurodevelopmental condition characterised by: (1) significant limitations in intellectual functioning (IQ below 70), (2) significant limitations in adaptive behaviour (communication, self-care, social skills), and (3) onset during the developmental period (before age 18).

**Classification by severity**:
- **Mild** (IQ 55–70): Can learn basic academic skills; largely independent as adults
- **Moderate** (IQ 40–55): Can learn self-care skills; need supported living
- **Severe** (IQ 25–40): Very limited communication; require significant support
- **Profound** (IQ below 25): Require total care

**Causes**: Chromosomal abnormalities (Down Syndrome — Trisomy 21), prenatal infections, birth complications (hypoxia), postnatal factors (severe malnutrition, head injury).

**Remedial Teaching Strategies**

1. **Task Analysis**: Break complex tasks into small, sequential steps
2. **Positive Reinforcement**: Immediate reward for correct responses; build motivation
3. **Repetition and Overlearning**: Repeated practice until skills are automatised
4. **Multisensory Instruction**: Use visual, auditory, and tactile materials simultaneously
5. **Concrete and Hands-On Learning**: Minimise abstract instruction; use real objects and demonstrations
6. **Functional Curriculum**: Focus on life skills — self-care, communication, community navigation
7. **Inclusive Education**: Where possible, educate children alongside typically developing peers with appropriate support
8. **Family Involvement**: Train parents to reinforce skills at home; maintain consistent routines
