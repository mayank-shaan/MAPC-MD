## 📝 Master Answer (250 words / 6 marks)

### Q. Elucidate the family life cycle during adulthood. (6 marks)

The **family life cycle** is a conceptual framework that describes the predictable stages a family unit passes through over time — from formation to dissolution. Understanding the family life cycle helps explain the changing roles, relationships, and challenges adults face across different life periods.

**Stages of the Family Life Cycle (Carter & McGoldrick)**

1. **Leaving Home: Single Young Adults** (late teens – mid-20s): Establishing independence from family of origin; forming personal identity; entering workforce.

2. **Joining of Families through Marriage/Commitment** (20s–30s): Forming a new couple system; renegotiating relationships with families of origin; establishing shared routines.

3. **Families with Young Children** (20s–40s): Adjusting marital relationship to make space for children; taking on parenting roles; renegotiating relationship with extended family as grandparents.

4. **Families with Adolescents** (35–55 years): Increasing flexibility of family boundaries to allow adolescents' independence; refocusing on midlife marital and career issues; caring for older generation.

5. **Launching Children and Moving On** (45–65 years): Children leaving home (empty nest); adjusting dyadic relationship; developing adult relationships with grown children; dealing with disabilities/deaths of parents.

6. **Families in Later Life** (60+ years): Accepting shift of generational roles; maintaining own/couple functioning; supporting the middle generation; coping with loss of spouse, siblings, peers.

**Attachment Patterns in Adulthood**

Bowlby's attachment theory extends into adult life — secure attachment in infancy predicts more satisfying adult romantic relationships. Hazan and Shaver (1987) applied attachment styles (secure, anxious, avoidant) to adult romantic relationships.
