## 📝 Master Answer (250 words / 6 marks)

### Q. Discuss gifted and talented children and the role of the teacher in their education. (6 marks)

**Gifted and Talented Children**

Gifted and talented children demonstrate exceptional abilities in one or more domains — intellectual, creative, artistic, leadership, or specific academic areas. Renzulli's Three-Ring Model defines giftedness as the intersection of: (1) above-average ability, (2) high creativity, and (3) high task commitment. Gardner's multiple intelligences framework recognises that giftedness can manifest in any of the eight intelligence domains.

**Characteristics of Gifted Children**

- Early and rapid language development
- Advanced reasoning and problem-solving abilities
- High curiosity and intrinsic motivation
- Long attention span for areas of interest
- Perfectionism and high personal standards
- May be socially mature or show asynchronous development (intellectually advanced but emotionally immature)
- May become bored or disruptive in a regular classroom not meeting their needs

**Identification**

Gifted children are identified through: standardised IQ tests (IQ above 130), teacher nominations, parent reports, portfolios of creative work, and performance on academic competitions.

**Role of the Teacher**

Teachers play a crucial role in supporting gifted children:
1. **Differentiated instruction**: Providing more complex, challenging material within the regular classroom
2. **Enrichment activities**: Projects, research, creative tasks that extend beyond the standard curriculum
3. **Acceleration options**: Advanced placement, grade skipping, or subject-specific acceleration
4. **Mentoring**: Connecting gifted students with experts and role models
5. **Emotional support**: Addressing perfectionism, social isolation, and asynchronous development
6. **Inclusive classroom**: Ensuring gifted children contribute to peer learning without alienating classmates

## 📝 Short Note — Gifted and Talented Children (100 words / 3 marks)

Gifted and talented children show exceptional abilities in intellectual, creative, or other domains. Key characteristics include advanced reasoning, high curiosity, rapid learning, strong intrinsic motivation, and perfectionism. Renzulli's Three-Ring Model defines giftedness as the intersection of above-average ability, creativity, and task commitment. Identification uses IQ tests (typically IQ > 130), teacher observation, and creative portfolios. Educational needs include differentiated instruction, enrichment activities, and possible academic acceleration. Without appropriate challenge, gifted children may become bored, disruptive, or underachieve. Teachers must balance providing challenge with supporting the child's social and emotional development.
