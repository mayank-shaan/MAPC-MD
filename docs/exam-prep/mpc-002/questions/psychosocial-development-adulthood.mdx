## 📝 Master Answer (250 words / 6 marks)

### Q. How does Erikson's and Levinson's theory highlight the psychosocial changes during early adulthood? (6 marks)

Early adulthood (approximately 18–40 years) is a period of establishing independence, forming intimate relationships, building careers, and starting families.

**Erikson's Stage 6: Intimacy vs Isolation**

The central psychosocial challenge of early adulthood, according to Erikson, is **Intimacy vs Isolation**. Having established a stable identity (Stage 5), the young adult now seeks deep, committed relationships. Intimacy involves the ability to fuse one's identity with another's in love, friendship, and close association without fear of losing oneself. Failure to achieve intimacy results in **isolation** — social withdrawal, superficial relationships, and a sense of loneliness. Successful resolution creates the virtue of **love** — mutual devotion in a committed relationship.

**Levinson's Seasons of Life in Early Adulthood**

Levinson (1978) described early adulthood as proceeding through:
- **Early Adult Transition** (17–22): Leaving the family of origin; entering the adult world tentatively
- **Entering the Adult World** (22–28): Establishing a life structure — career, relationships, lifestyle
- **Age 30 Transition** (28–33): Reassessing the provisional life structure; often involves stress and change
- **Settling Down** (33–40): Commitment to career, family, and "becoming one's own person"

Levinson emphasised the role of a **dream** (personal vision of adult achievement) and a **mentor** in early adulthood — both significantly influence the trajectory of adult development.

**Conclusion**: Erikson provides the emotional developmental core (intimacy), while Levinson maps the structural life changes (career, relationships, transitions) of early adulthood.
