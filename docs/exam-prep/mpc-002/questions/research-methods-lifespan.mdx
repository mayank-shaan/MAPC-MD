## 📝 Master Answer (250 words / 6 marks)

### Q. Discuss the various methods and obstacles in studying life span development. (6 marks)

**Research Methods in Lifespan Development**

1. **Cross-Sectional Method**: Different age groups are studied at the same point in time. Example: comparing 20-year-olds, 40-year-olds, and 60-year-olds simultaneously. **Advantage**: Quick and inexpensive. **Limitation**: Cohort effects — age groups grew up in different historical periods, so differences may reflect generational differences rather than development.

2. **Longitudinal Method**: The same individuals are studied repeatedly over a long period. **Advantage**: Shows genuine change within individuals; eliminates cohort effects. **Limitation**: Expensive, time-consuming, subject to attrition (dropout), and practice effects.

3. **Sequential (Cross-Sequential) Method**: Combines both approaches — multiple cohorts are followed longitudinally. Separates age effects, cohort effects, and time-of-measurement effects.

4. **Time Lag Method**: Compares same-aged individuals from different time periods (e.g., 30-year-olds in 1990 vs 30-year-olds in 2020). Used to study sociocultural change effects.

**Obstacles in Studying Lifespan Development**

1. **Ethical constraints**: Cannot experimentally manipulate human development
2. **Attrition**: Longitudinal studies lose participants over time — survivors may not be representative
3. **Cohort effects**: Cross-sectional comparisons confound age and generation
4. **Self-report limitations**: Retrospective recall of early life events is unreliable
5. **Cultural bias**: Most research conducted in Western, educated, industrialised, rich, democratic (WEIRD) contexts
6. **Multidisciplinary complexity**: Integrating biological, psychological, and social data is methodologically challenging
7. **Reactivity**: Repeated measurement may influence participants' behaviour
