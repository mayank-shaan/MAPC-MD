## 📝 Master Answer — Section A (450 words / 10 marks)

### Q. Discuss the psychosocial changes during middle adulthood, highlighting the developmental tasks during this stage. (10 marks)

**Introduction**

Middle adulthood, spanning approximately 40 to 60 years of age, is a period of significant psychosocial transition. Individuals in this stage typically occupy central positions in family and work life — as established professionals, parents of adolescents or young adults, and increasingly as caregivers to ageing parents. While physical capacities begin to decline, psychological complexity and accumulated wisdom often continue to grow. Erikson, Levinson, and Havighurst have each provided influential accounts of the psychosocial challenges of this stage.

**Erikson's Stage 7: Generativity vs Stagnation**

According to Erikson, the central psychosocial challenge of middle adulthood is **generativity vs stagnation**:

- **Generativity**: The desire to contribute meaningfully to the next generation — through parenting, mentoring, teaching, civic engagement, or creative work. Generative individuals feel a sense of purpose and legacy.
- **Stagnation**: The sense of having nothing meaningful to contribute; self-absorption and concern only with personal needs and comfort. Associated with emptiness, depression, and a sense of purposelessness.

Successful resolution of this conflict creates the virtue of **care** — an expanded concern for others that extends beyond the immediate family.

**Levinson's Seasons of Life**

Daniel Levinson (1978) described adult development as a sequence of alternating stable periods and transitional periods. In middle adulthood:

- The **Mid-Life Transition** (40–45 years) is a significant reassessment period — individuals question life choices, career paths, and relationships. For some, this becomes a dramatic "mid-life crisis" characterised by anxiety, impulsive decisions, or major life changes. For most, it is a reflective, gradual process.
- The subsequent stable period involves **restabilisation** — consolidating new priorities and commitments for the second half of adult life.

**Developmental Tasks of Middle Adulthood (Havighurst)**

Robert Havighurst identified several developmental tasks for middle adulthood:
1. Achieving civic and social responsibility
2. Establishing and maintaining an economic standard of living
3. Assisting teenage children to become responsible adults
4. Developing adult leisure activities
5. Relating to one's spouse as a person
6. Accepting and adjusting to the physiological changes of middle age
7. Adjusting to ageing parents

**Physical and Cognitive Changes**

Physical changes include decreased sensory acuity, hormonal changes (menopause in women; andropause in men), reduced stamina, and increased risk of chronic conditions. Cognitively, **fluid intelligence** declines while **crystallised intelligence** (accumulated knowledge and expertise) continues to grow.

**Conclusion**

Middle adulthood is a complex period that involves both losses and gains — physical decline but psychological depth, generational responsibility but expanding perspective. Successful navigation of this stage requires adaptability, continued investment in relationships, and the development of a generative orientation toward future generations.
