## 📝 Master Answer (250 words / 6 marks)

### Q. Discuss social development of children between 6–11 years of age. (6 marks)

Middle childhood (6–11 years) is characterised by a dramatic expansion of the social world. Peer relationships become central, school provides a new social arena, and children develop increasingly sophisticated social skills.

**Peer Relationships**

Peer groups become the primary social context during middle childhood. Key features:
- Peer groups are typically same-sex; children show strong in-group preferences
- **Peer acceptance** (sociometric status) becomes critical — popular, rejected, neglected, controversial, and average children are identified
- **Friendship** becomes more reciprocal and stable, based on shared interests and mutual trust (Sullivan, 1953)
- **Peer pressure** increases; children increasingly conform to peer norms

**School's Role**

School provides children with:
- Interactions with a diverse peer group
- Experience of structured competition and cooperation
- Development of industry vs inferiority (Erikson's Stage 4) — feeling competent and capable through mastery of school skills

**Erikson's Stage 4: Industry vs Inferiority**

The central psychosocial challenge of middle childhood. Children who successfully master school and social tasks develop a sense of **industry** (competence, confidence). Those who persistently fail develop a sense of **inferiority** (low self-esteem, inadequacy).

**Social Cognition**

Children in middle childhood develop improved **perspective-taking** (Selman's role-taking stages), enabling greater empathy and more sophisticated understanding of others' feelings and intentions. This supports both friendship quality and conflict resolution.

## 💡 Exam Tips
- Always mention **Erikson's Industry vs Inferiority** for middle childhood social development
- Include **peer acceptance** (sociometric status) — shows awareness of social psychology research
- For adolescence social development: shift focus to peer group conformity, identity formation through peers, and romantic relationships
