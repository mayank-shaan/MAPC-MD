## 📝 Master Answer (450 words / 10 marks)

### Q. Discuss adulthood and aging. What are the issues and challenges of aging in late adulthood? (10 marks)

**Introduction**

Ageing is an inevitable, universal process involving the gradual decline of biological, cognitive, and social functioning over time. Late adulthood (60+ years) encompasses a wide range of experiences — from active, healthy older adults to those managing multiple chronic conditions. Understanding the challenges of ageing is essential for developing appropriate support systems and promoting successful ageing.

**Biological Changes in Late Adulthood**

Physical changes associated with ageing include:
- Decreased sensory acuity (vision, hearing, taste, smell)
- Reduced muscle mass and bone density (leading to increased fracture risk)
- Slowing of cardiovascular and respiratory systems
- Weakened immune response
- Decline in motor coordination and reaction time

These physical changes can impact independence, self-confidence, and psychological well-being.

**Cognitive Changes**

- **Fluid intelligence** (speed, novel problem-solving) declines from middle adulthood
- **Crystallised intelligence** (accumulated knowledge, vocabulary) remains stable or improves into late adulthood
- **Memory**: Working memory and episodic memory show age-related decline; semantic and implicit memory are relatively preserved
- **Processing speed** slows — older adults take longer to process and respond
- Severe cognitive decline (dementia, Alzheimer's) affects a minority but is a significant concern

**Theories of Ageing**

1. **Disengagement Theory** (Cumming & Henry): Ageing involves mutual withdrawal — the elderly withdraw from society, and society from the elderly. Viewed this as natural and adaptive.
2. **Activity Theory**: Successful ageing requires maintaining activity and social engagement. Contradicts disengagement theory.
3. **Continuity Theory**: Older adults maintain consistent patterns from earlier life — successful ageing involves adapting these patterns, not abandoning them.

**Issues and Challenges in Late Adulthood**

1. **Retirement**: Loss of occupational identity, income reduction, need to restructure daily routine
2. **Health problems**: Managing chronic illness, disability, and dependency
3. **Social isolation**: Loss of work colleagues, friends, and spouse through death
4. **Economic insecurity**: Dependence on pension, family, or state support
5. **Ageism**: Social discrimination and stereotyping of older adults as incompetent or burdensome
6. **Housing and mobility**: Difficulty maintaining independent living; need for assisted care
7. **Grief and bereavement**: Multiple losses — friends, siblings, spouse — within a compressed time period

**Gender Differences in Ageing**

Women typically live longer than men but experience higher rates of disability, chronic illness, and widowhood in old age. Men tend to experience faster cognitive and physical decline after losing a spouse. Social roles also shape ageing differently — women's caregiving roles may continue into old age, while men's social networks shrink more rapidly after retirement.

**Conclusion**

Successful ageing requires addressing both individual and social dimensions — maintaining physical activity, cognitive engagement, meaningful relationships, and financial security. Public health interventions and family support systems play a critical role in enabling older adults to age with dignity and purpose.
