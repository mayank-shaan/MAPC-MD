## 📝 Master Answer — Characteristics & Psychosocial Development (450 words / 10 marks)

### Q. Discuss the major characteristics of infancy. Discuss psychosocial development during infancy. (10 marks)

**Introduction**

Infancy is the first stage of postnatal development, typically defined as the period from birth to approximately 2 years of age. It is a period of rapid and profound transformation — the infant progresses from a completely dependent newborn to a walking, talking, socially-responsive child. Infancy is characterised by dramatic physical growth, the emergence of cognitive abilities, and the formation of the first social bonds.

**Major Characteristics of Infancy**

1. **Rapid Physical Growth**: Infants triple their birth weight in the first year. Height increases by approximately 50% in the first year. Brain development is particularly rapid — the infant brain reaches approximately 75% of adult size by age 2.

2. **Reflexive Behaviour**: Newborns come equipped with survival reflexes — rooting, sucking, Moro (startle), grasp, and Babinski reflexes. Most disappear within the first few months as voluntary control develops.

3. **Perceptual Development**: Infants show preference for human faces from birth. Visual acuity improves rapidly; depth perception (evident by 6 months — "visual cliff" experiments). Hearing is functional before birth.

4. **Emergence of Voluntary Movement**: Cephalocaudal (head to tail) and proximodistal (centre to periphery) principles govern motor development. Milestones include head control (3 months), sitting (6 months), crawling (8–10 months), standing (11 months), walking (12–15 months).

5. **Rapid Cognitive Development**: The infant moves through Piaget's Sensorimotor Stage — from purely reflexive behaviour to intentional, goal-directed actions and the achievement of **object permanence** (understanding that objects continue to exist when out of sight, achieved around 8–12 months).

6. **Beginning of Language**: Vocalisations progress from crying (0–2 months) → cooing (2–3 months) → babbling (6 months) → single words (12 months) → two-word phrases (18–24 months).

**Psychosocial Development in Infancy**

Erikson's first psychosocial stage — **Trust vs Mistrust** — is the central psychosocial challenge of infancy. The resolution of this conflict depends entirely on the quality of caregiving:

- **Trust**: Develops when caregivers are consistently responsive, warm, and reliable in meeting the infant's needs. The infant learns that the world is a safe, predictable place — laying the foundation for healthy relationships and emotional security throughout life.

- **Mistrust**: Develops when caregivers are neglectful, inconsistent, or abusive. The infant learns to expect unreliability and danger — leading to anxiety, insecurity, and difficulty forming trust in later life.

**Attachment** (Bowlby, Ainsworth) develops within the first year as an emotional bond between infant and caregiver. Ainsworth's Strange Situation experiment identified four attachment patterns: secure, anxious-ambivalent, avoidant, and disorganised. Secure attachment — the healthiest pattern — is associated with sensitive, responsive caregiving.

**Hazards During Infancy**

Physical hazards include low birth weight, SIDS (Sudden Infant Death Syndrome), accidents, and failure-to-thrive. Psychological hazards include emotional deprivation (lack of nurturing), insecure attachment, and inadequate stimulation — all of which can have lasting effects on cognitive and emotional development.

**Conclusion**

Infancy is a foundational period. The quality of physical care and emotional nurturing during infancy has far-reaching consequences for cognitive development, emotional security, and social competence in later life.

## 🧠 Memory Aid

**Sensorimotor Sub-stages — "RFSP"** (simplified)
- **R**eflexes (0–1 month)
- **F**irst habits + circular reactions (1–4 months)
- **S**econdary circular reactions (4–8 months)
- **P**urposeful coordination + Object permanence (8–12 months)

**Trust vs Mistrust — Key factors**
- Consistent care → TRUST
- Neglectful/inconsistent care → MISTRUST

## 💡 Exam Tips

- Most infancy questions combine two sub-topics: (a) characteristics + (b) either psychosocial, cognitive, or linguistic development
- Always mention **Erikson's Trust vs Mistrust** for psychosocial development
- Always mention **Piaget's Sensorimotor Stage + object permanence** for cognitive development
- Mention **Bowlby and Ainsworth** for attachment — it strengthens the answer
- For Section B (250 words): Choose either physical characteristics OR psychosocial OR cognitive — don't try to cover everything in 250 words
