## 📝 Master Answer (250 words / 6 marks)

### Q. Explain motor development during early school years (6–11 years). (6 marks)

Motor development during middle childhood (6–11 years) is characterised by continuous refinement of both gross and fine motor skills, increased coordination, and growing physical competence.

**Gross Motor Development**

Gross motor skills involve large-muscle movements. Between ages 6–11, children show significant improvement in:
- **Running**: Faster and more coordinated; stride lengthens
- **Jumping and hopping**: Height and distance increase substantially
- **Throwing and catching**: More accurate and powerful
- **Sports participation**: Children can engage in organised team sports requiring complex motor coordination (football, cricket, swimming)

Boys typically show greater gross motor strength; girls show greater flexibility and balance. By age 10–11, children's gross motor performance approaches adult levels in many tasks.

**Fine Motor Development**

Fine motor skills involve small-muscle precision. Key developments:
- **Handwriting**: Becomes neater and faster; less effort required
- **Drawing**: More detailed and proportional
- **Using tools**: Scissors, rulers, compasses used with increasing accuracy

These improvements support school-based learning tasks.

**Physical Disorders and Hazards**

Some children in this age group experience motor development difficulties:
- **Developmental Coordination Disorder (DCD/Dyspraxia)**: Significant difficulty with motor coordination despite normal intelligence
- **Cerebral palsy**: Neurological condition affecting movement and posture
- Sports injuries become more common as physical activity intensity increases

**Conclusion**: Motor development in middle childhood underpins both physical and academic functioning. Physical education and structured play support continued motor refinement.

## 💡 Exam Tips
- Use the **gross vs fine motor** distinction as the main organising structure
- Mention **sex differences** in gross motor performance (boys: strength; girls: balance/flexibility)
- For early childhood motor development: focus on foundational skills (running, jumping, drawing) rather than sports
- Mention one motor disorder (DCD/dyspraxia) to add clinical depth
