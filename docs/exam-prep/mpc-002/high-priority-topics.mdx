## 📖 Detailed Topic Breakdown

### 1. Adolescence — 38 questions, 25/27 papers

**Why it's important:** The single most-asked topic in MPC-002. Appears in nearly every paper across all three sections. Questions range from general challenges to specific issues like sexuality, high-risk behaviour, and egocentrism.

**Key areas to study:**
- Challenges and hazards of adolescence
- High-risk behaviours and coping
- Sexuality acknowledgement and loss of normalcy
- Puberty, growth spurt, physical changes
- Adolescent egocentrism (Elkind)

**Resources:**
- [Questions & Answers](/exam-prep/mpc-002/questions/adolescence)
- [Adolescent Development Stages](/mpc-002/block-3/108-adolescent-development-stages)
- [Challenges & Issues in Adolescence](/mpc-002/block-3/120-challenges-issues-adolescent-development)
- [High-Risk Behaviours](/mpc-002/block-3/122-high-risk-behaviours-adolescence)

---

### 2. Piaget's Cognitive Development — 32 questions, 24/27 papers

**Why it's important:** Piaget's theory is asked across multiple developmental stages — middle childhood (concrete operational) and adolescence (formal operational). Questions appear in all sections.

**Key areas to study:**
- Concrete operational stage (6–11 years): conservation, decentration, classification
- Formal operational stage (adolescence): hypothetical reasoning, abstract thought
- Assimilation, accommodation, equilibration
- Comparison with Vygotsky

**Resources:**
- [Questions & Answers](/exam-prep/mpc-002/questions/piagets-cognitive-development)
- [Cognitive Development in Middle Childhood](/mpc-002/block-2/98-cognitive-development-middle-childhood)
- [Piaget's Formal Operations](/mpc-002/block-3/113-piaget-formal-operations)

---

### 3. Identity Development in Adolescence — 28 questions, 22/27 papers

**Why it's important:** THE most repeated specific question in the entire exam. Identity crisis and Marcia's identity statuses have appeared 15 times. This is a near-certain Section A or B question.

**Key areas to study:**
- Erikson's identity vs role confusion (Stage 5)
- Marcia's four identity statuses: Identity Achievement, Moratorium, Foreclosure, Identity Diffusion
- Identity crisis definition and process
- Self-concept vs self-esteem in adolescence
- Negative self-concept signs

**Resources:**
- [Questions & Answers](/exam-prep/mpc-002/questions/identity-development-adolescence)
- [Identity Development](/mpc-002/block-3/116-identity-development-adolescence)
- [Marcia's Identity Statuses](/mpc-002/block-3/117-marcia-identity-statuses)
- [Self-Concept & Self-Esteem](/mpc-002/block-3/118-self-concept-self-esteem-adolescence)

### 4. Prenatal Development — 22 questions, 18/27 papers

**Why it's important:** Consistently asked in Section A. Questions cover stages, environmental influences, and genetic factors.

**Key areas to study:**
- Three stages: germinal (zygote), embryonic, foetal
- Environmental influences: teratogens, nutrition, maternal age, stress
- Genetic influences and Rh incompatibility
- Characteristics of each prenatal stage

**Resources:**
- [Questions & Answers](/exam-prep/mpc-002/questions/prenatal-development)
- [Prenatal Stages of Development](/mpc-002/block-1/84-prenatal-stages-development)
- [Prenatal Characteristics & Environmental Influences](/mpc-002/block-1/85-prenatal-characteristics-environmental-influences)

### 5. Moral Development — 22 questions, 18/27 papers

**Why it's important:** The Piaget–Kohlberg comparison question has appeared 7+ times. Always prepare to compare both theories.

**Key areas to study:**
- Piaget's two stages: heteronomous vs autonomous morality
- Kohlberg's three levels and six stages
- Comparison of Piaget and Kohlberg
- Social cognition and prosocial behaviour
- Gilligan's critique of Kohlberg (if known)

**Resources:**
- [Questions & Answers](/exam-prep/mpc-002/questions/moral-development)
- [Moral Development in Middle Childhood](/mpc-002/block-2/100-moral-development-middle-childhood)

### 6. Ageing and Late Adulthood — 22 questions, 18/27 papers

**Why it's important:** Old age issues appear consistently. Questions typically ask about the ageing process, challenges, gender differences, and cognitive changes.

**Key areas to study:**
- Biological ageing theories
- Physical, cognitive, and psychosocial changes in late adulthood
- Gender differences in ageing
- Issues: retirement, health, loss, social isolation
- Successful ageing models

### 7. Infancy — 20 questions, 16/27 papers

**Why it's important:** Questions on infancy characteristics, hazards, and psychosocial development have appeared 8 times. The combined question (characteristics + psychosocial development) is a common Section A question.

**Key areas to study:**
- Characteristics of infancy (physical, cognitive, psychosocial)
- Physical and psychological hazards
- Erikson's Trust vs Mistrust (Stage 1)
- Cognitive development (sensorimotor stage)
- Language development in infancy
- Adjustments in infancy

### 8. Psychosocial Development in Late Adulthood — 20 questions, 17/27 papers

**Why it's important:** Overlaps with ageing topic but focuses specifically on psychosocial dimensions — Erikson's Stage 8, ego integrity, retirement, social roles.

**Key areas to study:**
- Erikson's Stage 8: Ego Integrity vs Despair
- Retirement and adjustment
- Social relationships in old age
- Widow(er)hood and bereavement
- Levinson's late adulthood period

**Resources:**
- [Questions & Answers](/exam-prep/mpc-002/questions/psychosocial-development-late-adulthood)

### 9. Learning Disability — 18 questions, 15/27 papers

**Why it's important:** Appears both as full questions and short notes. Questions consistently ask for identification, characteristics, and remedial programmes.

**Key areas to study:**
- Definition and types of learning disability (dyslexia, dyscalculia, dysgraphia)
- Characteristics and signs
- Identification process
- Remedial programmes and teaching strategies
- Role of teacher and family

### 10. Issues in Lifespan Development — 18 questions, 14/27 papers

**Why it's important:** Introductory concepts — lifespan perspectives, characteristics of development, issues like nature vs nurture — appear frequently in Section A and B.

**Key areas to study:**
- Lifespan perspective characteristics (multidirectional, multicultural, etc.)
- Major issues: nature vs nurture, stability vs change, continuity vs discontinuity
- Interacting forces (biological, psychological, sociocultural, life-cycle)
- Obstacles in studying lifespan development
- Domains of development

### 11. Information Processing Approach — 16 questions, 14/27 papers

**Why it's important:** This is most often asked with reference to cognitive development during adolescence. Repeated 7 times. A very predictable Section A question.

**Key areas to study:**
- Information processing model (input → processing → output)
- Attention, memory strategies, metacognition in adolescence
- Comparison with Piaget's stage theory
- Role of executive functions
- Implications for education

**Resources:**

### 12. Middle Adulthood — 16 questions, 14/27 papers

**Why it's important:** Developmental tasks, psychosocial changes, and Erikson's generativity vs stagnation are commonly asked.

**Key areas to study:**
- Developmental tasks of middle adulthood (Havighurst)
- Erikson's Stage 7: Generativity vs Stagnation
- Levinson's seasons of life (mid-life transition)
- Physical and cognitive changes
- Career changes and mid-life crisis

**Key areas to study:**

- Physical and psychological hazards
- Erikson's Initiative vs Guilt (Stage 3)

- Motor development in early childhood
