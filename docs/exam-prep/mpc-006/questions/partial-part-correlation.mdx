## 🎯 Must-Know Concepts
1. Partial correlation: correlation between X and Y with the effect of Z removed from **both**
2. Part (semi-partial) correlation: correlation between X and Y with the effect of Z removed from only one variable
3. When to use each
4. Formula and conceptual explanation

---

## 🔥 Most Repeated Question

**Common phrasings:**
- "Differentiate between partial and part correlation with suitable example."
- "Elucidate partial and part (semi-partial) correlation with the help of suitable examples."
- "Explain partial correlation. Compute Spearman's Rho for the following data."

### Answer (Section A or B — ~350 words):

When studying the relationship between two variables, a **third variable (control variable)** may be influencing both, creating a spurious or inflated correlation. Partial and part correlations control for this third variable.

**Partial Correlation (r_XY.Z):**

Partial correlation is the correlation between variables X and Y after **removing the effect of a third variable Z from both X and Y**.

*When to use*: When Z is related to both X and Y and you want to know the "pure" relationship between X and Y independent of Z.

*Example*: The correlation between vocabulary (X) and reading comprehension (Y) may be inflated because both are related to age (Z). The partial correlation r_XY.Z gives the relationship between vocabulary and reading when age is controlled.

*Formula (using zero-order correlations)*:
$$r_{XY.Z} = \frac{r_{XY} - r_{XZ} \cdot r_{YZ}}{\sqrt{(1 - r_{XZ}^2)(1 - r_{YZ}^2)}}$$

**Part (Semi-partial) Correlation (r_X(Y.Z)):**

Part correlation (also called semi-partial correlation) is the correlation between X and Y after **removing the effect of Z from only one variable (Y), not from both**.

*When to use*: When you want to know how much unique variance in X is explained by Y after removing the influence of Z from Y alone. Commonly used in multiple regression to assess unique contribution of each predictor.

*Example*: What is the unique relationship between study time (Y) and exam score (X), after removing the effect of intelligence (Z) from study time — but not from exam score?

**Key Difference:**

| Aspect | Partial Correlation | Part Correlation |
|--------|---------------------|-----------------|
| Z removed from | **Both** X and Y | **Only one** variable |
| Question answered | Pure X–Y relationship | Unique variance in X explained by Y alone |
| Use | Controlling confounds | Multiple regression, unique contribution |

Both partial and part correlations will be smaller than the zero-order (bivariate) correlation if Z is related to both variables.

## 🧠 Memory Aid

- **Partial**: Both variables **par**tially cleaned of Z
- **Part/Semi-partial**: Only **part** of the pair is cleaned (one variable only)
