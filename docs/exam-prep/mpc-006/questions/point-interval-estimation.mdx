## 🔥 Most Repeated Question

**Common phrasings:**
- "Discuss Point and Interval Estimation."
- "Point and Interval Estimation (short note)."

### Answer (Section B or C — ~200 words):

**Estimation** is the process of using sample statistics (such as the sample mean X̄) to estimate unknown population parameters (such as the population mean μ). Two types of estimation exist: point estimation and interval estimation.

**Point Estimation:**

A **point estimate** is a single numerical value computed from the sample and used as the best estimate of the corresponding population parameter.

*Examples*:
- Sample mean (X̄) is the point estimate of the population mean (μ)
- Sample standard deviation (s) is the point estimate of the population SD (σ)
- Sample proportion (p) is the point estimate of population proportion (P)

*Advantage*: Simple and easy to compute.

*Limitation*: Gives no information about how accurate the estimate is — no indication of the margin of error.

**Interval Estimation (Confidence Interval):**

An **interval estimate** (confidence interval) provides a range of values within which the population parameter is expected to fall with a specified degree of confidence.

*Formula for Confidence Interval for the Mean*:
$$CI = \bar{X} \pm Z_{\alpha/2} \times SE$$

or for small samples using t:
$$CI = \bar{X} \pm t_{\alpha/2} \times SE$$

Where SE = SD/√n

*Confidence levels*:
- **95% CI**: X̄ ± 1.96 × SE (we are 95% confident the population mean falls in this interval)
- **99% CI**: X̄ ± 2.58 × SE (wider interval, higher confidence)

*Interpretation*: A 95% confidence interval means that if we repeated sampling 100 times, approximately 95 of those intervals would contain the true population mean.

*Advantage over point estimation*: Provides a range that acknowledges sampling variability and quantifies our uncertainty.

*Trade-off*: Wider intervals give more confidence but are less precise. Narrower intervals are more precise but less confident.

---

## 🧠 Memory Aid

- **Point** estimate = **one** number (X̄ estimates μ)
- **Interval** estimate = a **range** (X̄ ± Z × SE)
- 95% CI uses Z = 1.96; 99% CI uses Z = 2.58
