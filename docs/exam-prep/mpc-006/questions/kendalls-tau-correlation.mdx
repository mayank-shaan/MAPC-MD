## 🎯 Must-Know Concepts
1. Kendall's tau (τ) is a non-parametric rank correlation coefficient
2. Based on concordant and discordant pairs
3. Formula: τ = (P − Q) / [n(n−1)/2]
4. When to use: ordinal data, or when comparing with Spearman's rho
5. Tau is generally lower in magnitude than Spearman's rho for the same data

---

## 🔥 Core Computation Procedure

### Step-by-Step Kendall's Tau

**Step 1: Rank both variables**
Assign ranks to both X and Y.

**Step 2: Arrange by rank of X**
Sort all pairs in ascending order of Rank X.

**Step 3: Count Concordant (P) and Discordant (Q) pairs**

For each pair in the sorted order, examine all pairs below it:
- **Concordant**: A pair below has a *higher* rank on Y → adds to P
- **Discordant**: A pair below has a *lower* rank on Y → adds to Q

Sum all concordant pairs → P  
Sum all discordant pairs → Q

**Step 4: Apply the formula**
$$\tau = \frac{P - Q}{\frac{n(n-1)}{2}}$$

Where n(n−1)/2 = total number of pairs compared.

**Step 5: Interpret**
- τ = +1: Perfect positive concordance
- τ = −1: Perfect negative concordance (all discordant)
- τ = 0: No relationship

**Step 6: Significance test** (for larger samples, convert to z-score)

## 📝 Conceptual: Kendall's Tau vs Spearman's Rho (Section B — ~250 words)

| Aspect | Kendall's Tau (τ) | Spearman's Rho (ρ) |
|--------|------------------|-------------------|
| **Basis** | Concordant and discordant pairs | Rank differences (D²) |
| **Formula** | (P−Q) / [n(n−1)/2] | 1 − 6ΣD²/n(n²−1) |
| **Range** | −1 to +1 | −1 to +1 |
| **Value** | Generally lower than rho for same data | Generally higher than tau |
| **Interpretation** | Probability that a randomly chosen pair is concordant minus discordant | Degree of monotonic rank relationship |
| **Preferred** | Smaller samples, ties present | Standard rank correlation |

**When to use Kendall's Tau:**
1. When data consists of natural ranks or ordinal measurements
2. When sample is small
3. When you want a probability-based interpretation (the difference in probability of concordant vs discordant pairs)
4. When there are many ties in the data

Both tau and rho are equivalent to Pearson's r when the data are bivariate normally distributed and the relationship is linear.
