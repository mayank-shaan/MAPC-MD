### Answer (Section A — ~450 words):

The **Normal Distribution** (also called the Gaussian distribution) is a symmetric, bell-shaped theoretical frequency distribution that describes how many naturally occurring variables are distributed in large populations. The **Normal Probability Curve (NPC)** is the graphical representation of the normal distribution.

**Concept:**
The NPC was developed by the mathematician Gauss and is sometimes called the Gaussian curve. It arises when a large number of independent random factors contribute to a measurement, making it the most common distribution encountered in psychological and educational research.

**Diagram:**
The curve is bell-shaped, perfectly symmetrical around the mean, tapering off gradually at both ends (asymptotic to the horizontal axis).

**Properties/Characteristics of the Normal Probability Curve:**

1. *Bell-shaped and symmetrical*: The curve is perfectly symmetrical about the mean. The left and right halves are mirror images.

2. *Mean, median, and mode coincide*: All three measures of central tendency fall at exactly the same point — the centre of the distribution.

3. *Asymptotic*: The tails of the curve approach but never touch the horizontal axis, extending to ±infinity. This implies that even very extreme scores have a small but non-zero probability.

4. *Unimodal*: The curve has only one peak (mode) at the centre.

5. *Total area = 1*: The total area under the curve represents all probabilities and equals 1.00 (or 100%).

6. *Fixed area proportions (Empirical Rule)*:
   - Within ±1σ: 68.26% of cases
   - Within ±2σ: 95.44% of cases
   - Within ±3σ: 99.72% of cases

7. *Points of inflection*: The curve changes from concave to convex at exactly ±1 standard deviation from the mean.

8. *Mathematically defined*: The curve is fully defined by just two parameters — the mean (μ) and standard deviation (σ).

**Importance and Applications of NPC in Psychology:**

1. *Standardisation of tests*: Most psychological tests (IQ tests, aptitude tests, personality inventories) are standardised assuming a normal distribution of scores.

2. *Statistical inference*: Many inferential statistical tests (t-test, ANOVA, Pearson's r) assume normally distributed data. The NPC provides the theoretical basis for these tests.

3. *Converting raw scores*: The normal curve allows conversion of raw scores to standard scores (z-scores), percentile ranks, and stanines, making comparison across different tests possible.

4. *Research in psychology and education*: Traits such as intelligence, height, and reaction time are approximately normally distributed in the population, making the NPC directly applicable to psychological research.

5. *Determining probability*: Areas under the NPC correspond to probabilities, enabling researchers to determine the probability of obtaining a given score by chance.

## 📝 Other Key Questions

### Area under the Normal Curve (Section B — ~250 words)

The area under the NPC is related to z-scores (standard scores). A z-score is computed as:
$$z = \frac{X - \mu}{\sigma}$$

Key areas:
- Between μ and +1σ: 34.13% → Total ±1σ: **68.26%**
- Between μ and +2σ: 47.72% → Total ±2σ: **95.44%**
- Between μ and +3σ: 49.86% → Total ±3σ: **99.72%**

These fixed proportions allow psychologists to interpret how extreme or common any score is. For example, in an IQ test (mean = 100, SD = 15), a score of 130 is exactly 2 SDs above the mean — placing it in the top 2.28% of the population.
