## 🔥 Core Computation Procedure (Section A — ~450 words)

**Question type**: "Compute one-way ANOVA for the following data and indicate whether the groups differ significantly."

### Step-by-Step ANOVA Computation

**Given**: Scores from k groups (typically 3 groups with n subjects each)

**Step 1: Calculate the Grand Total and Grand Mean**
- Grand Total (ΣX) = Sum of all scores
- Grand Mean (X̄G) = ΣX / N (N = total subjects across all groups)

**Step 2: Calculate Group Totals and Means**
- For each group j: Tj = ΣXj, X̄j = Tj / nj

**Step 3: Calculate Sum of Squares (SS)**

*SS Total* (total variability):
$$SS_{Total} = \sum X^2 - \frac{(\sum X)^2}{N}$$

*SS Between* (variability due to group differences):
$$SS_{Between} = \sum_j \frac{T_j^2}{n_j} - \frac{(\sum X)^2}{N}$$

*SS Within* (variability within each group):
$$SS_{Within} = SS_{Total} - SS_{Between}$$

**Step 4: Degrees of Freedom (df)**
- df Between = k − 1 (k = number of groups)
- df Within = N − k
- df Total = N − 1

**Step 5: Mean Squares (MS)**
- MS Between = SS Between / df Between
- MS Within = SS Within / df Within

**Step 6: F-ratio**
$$F = \frac{MS_{Between}}{MS_{Within}}$$

**Step 7: ANOVA Summary Table**

| Source | SS | df | MS | F |
|--------|----|----|----|----|
| Between Groups | SS_B | k−1 | MS_B | MS_B/MS_W |
| Within Groups | SS_W | N−k | MS_W | — |
| Total | SS_T | N−1 | — | — |

**Step 8: Decision**
- Find F critical from F-distribution table at df1 = k−1, df2 = N−k at α = 0.05
- If F obtained ≥ F critical: Reject H₀ → Groups differ significantly
- If F obtained < F critical: Retain H₀ → Groups do not differ significantly

### Null and Alternative Hypothesis
- **H₀**: μ₁ = μ₂ = μ₃ (all group means are equal)
- **H₁**: At least one group mean is different

## 📝 Conceptual Questions

### Assumptions of ANOVA (Section B — ~250 words)

ANOVA rests on several assumptions:

1. *Normality*: Scores within each group are normally distributed. ANOVA is relatively robust to violations of normality when sample sizes are equal and reasonably large.
2. *Homogeneity of variance*: The variance within each group is approximately equal (homoscedasticity). Levene's test can be used to check this assumption.
3. *Independence of observations*: Scores from different subjects are independent of each other.
4. *Random sampling*: Participants are randomly assigned to groups (in true experiments).

*Advantages of ANOVA:*
- Can compare three or more groups simultaneously, avoiding the inflation of Type I error that occurs with multiple t-tests.
- Partitions total variability into meaningful components (between-group and within-group).
- Extensible to complex designs (two-way ANOVA, mixed designs).

*Limitations of ANOVA:*
- Assumes normality and homogeneity of variance.
- Only tells us that at least one group differs; does not identify which groups differ (post-hoc tests needed).
- Sensitive to outliers.
