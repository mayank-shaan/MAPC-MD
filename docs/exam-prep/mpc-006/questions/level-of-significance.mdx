## 🔥 Most Repeated Question

**Common phrasings:**
- "Describe steps in setting up the level of significance."
- "Delineate the steps in setting up the level of significance."
- "Level of significance (short note)."
- "Discuss the concept of level of significance."

### Answer (Section B or C — ~200 words):

**Level of Significance (α)** is the probability value at which the researcher decides to reject the null hypothesis. It defines the boundary between "statistically significant" and "not statistically significant" results.

**Definition**: The level of significance is the maximum probability of committing a Type I error (rejecting a true null hypothesis) that the researcher is willing to accept. Conventionally, α = 0.05 (5%) or α = 0.01 (1%).

**Steps in Setting Up the Level of Significance:**

1. *State the null hypothesis (H₀)*: Clearly define what "no effect" or "no difference" means in the context of the study.

2. *Choose the level of significance (α)*: Based on the consequences of Type I error. Use α = 0.01 when a false positive would be costly (medical research, legal contexts). Use α = 0.05 for most behavioural and social science research.

3. *Determine whether the test is one-tailed or two-tailed*: A directional prediction uses one-tailed test (entire α in one tail). A non-directional prediction uses two-tailed test (α/2 in each tail).

4. *Identify degrees of freedom (df)*: df depends on sample size and the type of test.

5. *Find the critical value*: Using the appropriate statistical table (z, t, F, χ²) at the chosen α and df.

6. *Define the critical region*: The area beyond the critical value in the tail(s) of the sampling distribution. Any obtained statistic falling in this region leads to rejection of H₀.

**Relationship with Type I Error:**
α directly equals the probability of Type I error. Lowering α (e.g., from 0.05 to 0.01) reduces the risk of falsely rejecting H₀, but increases the risk of Type II error.

**Confidence Level**: If α = 0.05, the confidence level = 1 − α = 0.95 (95%). This means we are 95% confident in retaining correct decisions.

---

## 🧠 Memory Aid

**α = 0.05**: In 100 hypothesis tests, we expect to commit a Type I error about 5 times by chance alone, even if H₀ is always true.
