### Skewness (Section C — ~100 words):

**Skewness** is a measure of the asymmetry of a frequency distribution. A perfectly normal distribution has zero skewness.

*Positive Skew (Right skew)*: The tail extends to the right; most scores cluster at the lower end. Mean > Median > Mode.

*Negative Skew (Left skew)*: The tail extends to the left; most scores cluster at the higher end. Mean < Median < Mode.

Skewness can be caused by ceiling effects, floor effects, extreme outliers, or the natural properties of the variable being measured.

### Kurtosis (Section C — ~100 words):

**Kurtosis** is a measure of the **peakedness** (degree of flatness or pointedness) of a frequency distribution relative to the normal curve.

*Leptokurtic*: More peaked than normal; tall and narrow with heavier tails than a normal distribution. Excess kurtosis > 0.

*Platykurtic*: Flatter than normal; scores spread more evenly across the range. Excess kurtosis < 0.

*Mesokurtic*: Same peakedness as the normal distribution. Excess kurtosis = 0.

Kurtosis affects the reliability of statistical tests that assume normality — extreme kurtosis violates those assumptions.

### Combined Answer for Section B (~250 words):

**Skewness and Kurtosis** are two key ways in which empirical distributions diverge from the ideal normal curve.

**Skewness** measures **asymmetry**. When the left and right halves of the distribution are not mirror images, the distribution is skewed:
- *Positive skew*: Long right tail. Mean is pulled toward the high scores: Mean > Median > Mode. Example: Income distribution (most people earn moderately, a few earn extremely high amounts).
- *Negative skew*: Long left tail. Mean is pulled toward low scores: Mean < Median < Mode. Example: Age at death in developed countries (most die in old age, few die young).

**Measuring Skewness**: Pearson's coefficient of skewness = 3(Mean − Median) / SD. A value of 0 = symmetrical.

**Kurtosis** measures **peakedness**:
- *Leptokurtic*: Tall, narrow peak; heavy tails. Scores are concentrated near the mean. Examples: distributions of test scores in homogeneous, well-trained groups.
- *Platykurtic*: Flat, broad distribution; light tails. Scores are widely spread. Example: uniform distributions.
- *Mesokurtic*: Normal kurtosis. The baseline against which others are compared.

**Diagrams** are essential: draw all three kurtosis types on one graph and both skew types clearly labelled.

Both skewness and kurtosis inform decisions about which statistical tests to use. Highly skewed or kurtotic data may require non-parametric tests or data transformation before applying parametric analyses.
