## 🔥 Most Repeated Question

**Common phrasings:**
- "Describe the measures of central tendency and measures of dispersion."
- "Describe measures of central tendency. Elucidate advantages and disadvantages of descriptive statistics."
- "Differentiate between descriptive and inferential statistics."

### Answer (Section A — ~450 words):

**Descriptive statistics** summarise and describe the characteristics of a dataset. They include measures of central tendency (where scores cluster) and measures of dispersion (how spread out scores are).

**Measures of Central Tendency:**

1. *Mean (Arithmetic Mean)*: The sum of all scores divided by the number of scores. X̄ = ΣX/n.
   - *Advantages*: Uses all data; algebraically tractable; basis for further statistical analysis.
   - *Limitations*: Affected by extreme scores (outliers); inappropriate for skewed distributions.
   - *Use*: Interval and ratio data, approximately normal distributions.

2. *Median*: The middle score when scores are arranged in order. If n is even, median = average of two middle scores.
   - *Advantages*: Not affected by extreme scores; suitable for skewed distributions.
   - *Limitations*: Does not use all data; less suitable for further algebraic operations.
   - *Use*: Ordinal data, skewed distributions.

3. *Mode*: The most frequently occurring score. A distribution can be unimodal, bimodal, or multimodal.
   - *Advantages*: Unaffected by extreme scores; only measure for nominal data.
   - *Limitations*: May not exist; multiple modes possible; unstable in small samples.
   - *Use*: Nominal data, describing most typical value.

**Measures of Dispersion:**

Measures of dispersion indicate the spread or variability of scores around the central value.

1. *Range*: Highest score − Lowest score.
   - Simple but unstable; heavily influenced by extreme scores.

2. *Variance (σ² or s²)*: The average of squared deviations from the mean.
   $$\sigma^2 = \frac{\Sigma(X - \bar{X})^2}{n}$$
   - Includes all scores; basis of many inferential statistics.
   - Expressed in squared units — harder to interpret directly.

3. *Standard Deviation (SD or σ)*: Square root of the variance. Most widely used measure of dispersion.
   $$SD = \sqrt{\frac{\Sigma(X-\bar{X})^2}{n}}$$
   - Same units as the original data; easy to interpret.
   - Basis for z-scores, standard error, and most parametric tests.

**Descriptive vs Inferential Statistics:**
- *Descriptive statistics* summarise data from the sample itself (mean, SD, frequency tables, graphs).
- *Inferential statistics* use sample data to make inferences about the population (t-test, ANOVA, correlation significance).

---

## 🧠 Memory Aid

**Central tendency**: Mean (equal intervals, no outliers), Median (skewed data), Mode (nominal).

**Dispersion**: Range (quick, rough), Variance (precise but squared units), SD (precise, same units).
