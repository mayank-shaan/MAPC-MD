## 🎯 Must-Know Concepts
1. Mann-Whitney U is the non-parametric alternative to the independent samples t-test
2. Used when data is ordinal or not normally distributed
3. Based on ranking all scores together from both groups
4. Two U values computed: U₁ and U₂; the smaller is used
5. Decision: compare U obtained with U critical (U obtained ≤ U critical = significant)

---

## 🔥 Core Computation Procedure

### Step-by-Step Mann-Whitney U Computation

**Step 1: State H₀ and H₁**
- H₀: There is no significant difference between Group 1 and Group 2
- H₁: There is a significant difference between the two groups

**Step 2: Rank all scores together**
Combine all scores from both groups, then rank them from lowest (rank 1) to highest. In case of ties, assign average ranks.

**Step 3: Compute U₁ and U₂**

$$U_1 = n_1 n_2 + \frac{n_1(n_1+1)}{2} - R_1$$

$$U_2 = n_1 n_2 + \frac{n_2(n_2+1)}{2} - R_2$$

Where:
- n₁ = number of subjects in Group 1
- n₂ = number of subjects in Group 2
- R₁ = sum of ranks for Group 1
- R₂ = sum of ranks for Group 2

**Verification**: U₁ + U₂ = n₁ × n₂

**Step 4: Select the smaller U**
U = smaller of U₁ and U₂

**Step 5: Find U critical from the Mann-Whitney U table**
Look up critical U at n₁ and n₂ at α = 0.05 (two-tailed)

**Step 6: Decision**
- If U obtained ≤ U critical: Reject H₀ → Significant difference exists
- If U obtained > U critical: Retain H₀ → No significant difference

> **Note**: For Mann-Whitney U, the decision rule is reversed from most tests — the obtained value must be **smaller** than or equal to the critical value to be significant.

### Worked Example Layout

| All Scores Combined | Group | Rank |
|---------------------|-------|------|
| Score 1 | G1 | 1 |
| Score 2 | G2 | 2 |
| ... | ... | ... |

Then compute R₁, R₂, U₁, U₂.

## 📝 Conceptual Questions

### When to use Mann-Whitney U Test (Section B — ~250 words)

The Mann-Whitney U test is used when:
1. The data are measured on an ordinal scale or the continuous data violates normality assumptions
2. The researcher is comparing **two independent groups**
3. Sample sizes are small
4. The independent samples t-test assumptions cannot be met

**Relationship to t-test**: The Mann-Whitney U test is the non-parametric alternative to the independent samples t-test. Where the t-test requires interval/ratio data and assumes normality, the Mann-Whitney U test requires only ordinal-level data. When the normality assumption is met, the t-test is more powerful; when it is violated, Mann-Whitney U is preferred.

**Assumptions of Mann-Whitney U:**
1. Two independent groups
2. Data measured at least on ordinal scale
3. Observations are independent

- Verify your work: U₁ + U₂ must equal n₁ × n₂.
- State H₀ before computing.
- Remember: smaller U = more significant. The decision rule is the opposite of most tests.
- Mention that Mann-Whitney U is the non-parametric equivalent of the t-test.
