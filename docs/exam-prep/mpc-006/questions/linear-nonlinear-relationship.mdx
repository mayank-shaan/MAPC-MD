## 🎯 Must-Know Concepts
1. Linear relationship: constant rate of change; scatter plot fits a straight line
2. Non-linear relationship: variable rate; scatter plot follows a curve
3. Positive and negative linear relationships
4. Pearson's r only valid for linear relationships
5. Outliers, curvilinearity, and their effect on correlation

### Answer (Section A conceptual component — ~200 words):

**Linear Relationship:**

A **linear relationship** between two variables X and Y exists when the change in Y is proportional to the change in X at a constant rate. When plotted on a scatter diagram, the data points cluster around a straight line.

*Types*:
- *Positive linear relationship*: As X increases, Y increases proportionally. The regression line slopes upward (e.g., study hours and exam scores).
- *Negative linear relationship*: As X increases, Y decreases proportionally. The regression line slopes downward (e.g., anxiety level and performance quality).

Pearson's product moment correlation coefficient (r) and linear regression are appropriate measures for linear relationships.

**Non-linear Relationship:**

A **non-linear (curvilinear) relationship** exists when the change in Y is not proportional to the change in X. The scatter plot follows a curved pattern rather than a straight line.

*Example*: The Yerkes-Dodson law describes an inverted U-shaped relationship between arousal (X) and performance (Y). Performance increases with moderate arousal but decreases at both very low and very high arousal levels. Pearson's r would underestimate this relationship (giving r near 0).

*Implication*: If Pearson's r ≈ 0, it does not necessarily mean no relationship — it may indicate a non-linear relationship. Visual inspection of the scatter plot is essential.

**Outliers:**

Outliers are extreme data points that deviate markedly from the rest of the distribution. They can artificially inflate or deflate Pearson's r. Spearman's rho is less sensitive to outliers because it uses ranks.

---

## 🧠 Memory Aid

- **Linear** = straight line on scatter plot → Pearson's r is appropriate
- **Non-linear** = curved pattern → Pearson's r underestimates; use eta (η) or Spearman's rho
