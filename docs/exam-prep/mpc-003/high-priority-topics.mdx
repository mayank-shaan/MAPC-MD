## 📖 Topic Summaries

### 1. Behavioural Assessment *(8.0% — Most Repeated)*
The single most tested topic. Appears in almost every paper, often in Section A as a full 10-mark question.
**Must know**: Definition, common characteristics, assumptions, types (observation, interview, case study, situational tests), advantages, limitations.

### 2. Social Learning Theory — Bandura *(7.6%)*
Asked as a full Section A question in 12 papers. Includes: observational learning, vicarious reinforcement, self-efficacy, reciprocal determinism, self-system.
**Must know**: Principles of observational learning, modelling process (attention, retention, reproduction, motivation), self-efficacy sources.

### 3. Allport's Theory *(7.1%)*
Appears across all sections. Sub-topics: trait theory, proprium (selfhood), functional autonomy, mature personality, personal dispositions (cardinal, central, secondary).
**Must know**: 8 aspects of proprium, functional autonomy types, 7 characteristics of mature personality.

### 4. Projective Techniques *(7.1%)*
Asked in Sections A, B, and C. Sub-topics: definition, classification (Lindsay's), strengths, weaknesses, specific tests (Rorschach, TAT, CAT, inkblot).
**Must know**: Lindsay's 5 categories (association, construction, completion, arrangement, expressive), strengths and weaknesses.

### 5. Personality: Definition and Determinants *(6.7%)*
Almost always combined — "Define personality AND discuss biological/psychological factors." Both parts needed.
**Must know**: Major definitions (Allport, Eysenck), biological factors (heredity, body build, nervous system), psychological factors (self-concept, motivation, emotions).

### 6. Eysenck's Theory *(6.2%)*
Type-trait hierarchy, three dimensions (E, N, P), neurophysiological basis of extraversion and neuroticism, PEN model.
**Must know**: Hierarchical model (specific responses → habitual → traits → types), biological basis (cortical arousal for E, limbic system for N).

### 7. Cattell's Trait Theory *(6.2%)*
Formula for personality (R = f(S, P)), categories of traits (surface/source, common/unique, ability/temperament/dynamic, constitutional/environmental-mould), 16PF.
**Must know**: Source vs surface traits distinction, L/Q/T data, 16 primary factors.

### 8. Classical Conditioning / Pavlov *(6.2%)*
Principles: acquisition, extinction, spontaneous recovery, generalisation, discrimination, higher-order conditioning.
**Must know**: All key principles with examples, application to personality development.

### 9. Five Factor Model / Big Five *(5.3%)*
OCEAN acronym — Openness, Conscientiousness, Extraversion, Agreeableness, Neuroticism. Measurement via NEO-PI-R and BFI.
**Must know**: Description of each factor, high vs low scorers, measurement methods, criticism.

### 10. Freud's Psychoanalytic Theory *(5.8%)*
Structural model (id, ego, superego), psychosexual stages (oral, anal, phallic, latency, genital), defense mechanisms, topographic model.
**Must know**: All 5 psychosexual stages with fixation consequences, major defense mechanisms.

### 11. Maslow's Hierarchy / Humanistic Theory *(5.3%)*
Hierarchy of needs (5 levels), deficiency vs growth needs, characteristics of self-actualizers, peak experiences.
**Must know**: All 5 levels, D-needs vs B-needs, 15 characteristics of self-actualizers (Maslow), comparison with Rogers.

### 12. Rogers' Theory *(4.4%)*
Person-centered theory, self-concept (real vs ideal self), conditions of worth, unconditional positive regard, fully functioning person.
**Must know**: Organismic valuing process, congruence vs incongruence, 7 characteristics of fully functioning person.

### 13. Personality Assessment: Meaning, Purpose & Criteria *(4.4%)*
Definition of personality assessment, purposes (clinical, research, selection), technical criteria (standardisation, reliability, validity, norms).
**Must know**: Types of reliability (test-retest, split-half), types of validity (content, criterion, construct), standardisation process.

### 14. Self-Report Inventories *(4.4%)*
Types (MMPI, NEO-PI, 16PF, CPI, EPQ), strengths (economical, standardised), weaknesses (faking, social desirability, response sets), methods to overcome weaknesses.
**Must know**: Main inventories and what they measure, faking good/bad, forced-choice format, validity scales in MMPI.
