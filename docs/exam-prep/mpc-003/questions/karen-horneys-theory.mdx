## 📝 Master Answer — Section A (450 words / 10 marks)

### Q. Explain Karen Horney's theory of personality. (10 marks)

*Also: "Explain the concept of basic anxiety and neurotic needs." (2020-Jun); "Discuss the patterns of neurotic needs as described by Karen Horney." (2014-Dec)*

**Introduction**

Karen Horney (1885–1952) was a neo-Freudian who challenged Freudian orthodoxy — particularly its biological determinism and masculine bias — while retaining the centrality of unconscious processes. She argued that personality is shaped primarily by **social and cultural factors**, particularly early parent-child relationships. Her theory centres on basic anxiety and the neurotic strategies people develop to cope with it.

**Basic Anxiety and Basic Hostility**

Horney argued that children raised in environments that are cold, indifferent, overprotective, inconsistent, or abusive develop **basic anxiety** — a pervasive feeling of being alone and helpless in a potentially hostile world. Alongside this, the child develops **basic hostility** toward the parents who fail to provide safety. However, expressing this hostility is dangerous (it risks losing the little love available), so the child represses it — which deepens the anxiety.

**Ten Neurotic Needs (Coping Strategies)**

To manage basic anxiety, individuals develop neurotic needs — compulsive, insatiable strategies for achieving security. Horney identified ten such needs, which she later organised into three broad coping orientations:

**1. Moving Toward People (Compliant/Self-Effacing Personality)**
Needs: affection and approval; a dominant partner; living within narrow limits.
These individuals cope by clinging to others, being excessively agreeable, and submerging their own needs.

**2. Moving Against People (Expansive/Aggressive Personality)**
Needs: power; exploitation of others; social recognition; admiration; personal achievement.
These individuals cope by dominating, competing, and controlling others.

**3. Moving Away from People (Resigned/Detached Personality)**
Needs: self-sufficiency and independence; perfection and unassailability.
These individuals cope by withdrawing emotionally, maintaining distance, and avoiding commitment.

**The Neurotic Conflict**

Healthy individuals move flexibly among all three orientations as situations demand. Neurotic individuals are rigid — compulsively committed to one orientation regardless of context. The conflict between incompatible orientations within the same person creates neurotic suffering.

**The Idealised Self and Real Self**

Horney also described how neurotics develop an **idealised self-image** — an inflated, unrealistic picture of themselves that provides a substitute for genuine self-esteem. Striving to live up to this idealised image creates a tyranny of the "should": "I should be perfect," "I should be admired." The gap between the idealised self and the real self generates self-contempt.

**Conclusion**

Horney's theory offers a socially sensitive account of neurosis that emphasises cultural and relational determinants over biology. Her concept of basic anxiety, neurotic needs, and the three coping orientations remains clinically useful and is echoed in contemporary attachment and interpersonal theories of psychopathology.

## 🧠 Memory Aid

**Three orientations**: "**TAA**" — **T**oward, **A**gainst, **A**way  
**Neurotic needs by orientation**:
- *Toward* (3): Affection, Partner, Narrow limits
- *Against* (5): Power, Exploitation, Recognition, Admiration, Achievement
- *Away* (2): Self-sufficiency, Perfection

---

## 💡 Exam Tips

- The **three coping orientations** (toward/against/away) are more important to know than all ten individual needs.
- Always mention that healthy flexibility vs neurotic rigidity is the key distinction.
- Horney's critique of Freud (womb envy > penis envy, cultural factors over biology) can earn extra marks in Section A.
