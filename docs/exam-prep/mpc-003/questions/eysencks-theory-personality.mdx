## 📝 Master Answer — Section A (450 words / 10 marks)

### Q. Define Personality. Discuss in detail Eysenck's type/trait hierarchy. (10 marks)

*Also: "Elucidate the dimensions of personality as given by Eysenck." (2024-Dec); "Elaborate Eysenck's theory of personality." (2019-Jun)*

**Introduction**

Hans Eysenck (1916–1997), a British psychologist, developed one of the most influential biologically-grounded trait theories of personality. Synthesising factor analysis with experimental psychology and biological research, Eysenck argued that personality is rooted in the nervous system and can be described at multiple hierarchical levels. His theory is notable for being both descriptive (identifying personality dimensions) and explanatory (providing neurophysiological mechanisms).

**Eysenck's Hierarchical Model**

Eysenck organised personality into four hierarchical levels:

1. **Specific Response Level**: Individual, specific behaviours or reactions in a particular situation (e.g., feeling nervous before an exam)
2. **Habitual Response Level**: Habitual behaviours that recur in similar situations (e.g., consistently being nervous in evaluative contexts)
3. **Trait Level**: Clusters of correlated habitual responses that form recognisable traits (e.g., shyness, low confidence)
4. **Type Level**: Broad super-factors formed by clusters of correlated traits (e.g., Introversion as a type encompassing shyness, social withdrawal, preference for solitude)

**Three Super-Factor Dimensions (PEN Model)**

Through factor analysis of personality questionnaire data, Eysenck identified three major dimensions:

1. **Extraversion–Introversion (E)**
   - *Extraversion*: Sociable, lively, optimistic, sensation-seeking, carefree
   - *Introversion*: Quiet, reserved, reliable, controlled, pessimistic
   - *Neurophysiological basis*: Linked to **cortical arousal**. Introverts have higher baseline cortical arousal (via ascending reticular activating system — ARAS), so they seek stimulation reduction. Extraverts have lower baseline arousal and seek external stimulation to reach optimal level.

2. **Neuroticism–Stability (N)**
   - *High Neuroticism*: Anxious, moody, emotionally labile, easily upset
   - *Emotional Stability*: Calm, even-tempered, controlled, reliable
   - *Neurophysiological basis*: Linked to **limbic system reactivity** (visceral brain). High-N individuals have a more reactive limbic system, making them emotionally volatile and slow to return to baseline after arousal.

3. **Psychoticism (P)**
   - *High Psychoticism*: Aggressive, cold, egocentric, impulsive, antisocial, creative
   - *Low Psychoticism*: Empathic, cooperative, conventional
   - Linked to **testosterone levels** and **dopaminergic activity**. High P is associated with schizophrenia and psychopathy spectrum.

**The Eysenck Personality Questionnaire (EPQ)**

Eysenck developed the Maudsley Personality Inventory (MPI), the Eysenck Personality Inventory (EPI), and the Eysenck Personality Questionnaire (EPQ) to measure these dimensions. The EPQ includes a Lie scale to detect social desirability responding.

**Criminal and Clinical Applications**

Eysenck predicted that high E + high N individuals would be more prone to antisocial behaviour, as their nervous systems are harder to condition. His theory linked personality dimensions to differential conditionability and thus to the development of neuroses and personality disorders.

**Conclusion**

Eysenck's type-trait hierarchy represents a landmark achievement in personality psychology — providing a parsimonious, empirically testable, and biologically grounded account of individual differences. Its integration of factor-analytic methodology with experimental neuroscience established the template for modern biological approaches to personality.
