## 📝 Master Answer — Section A (450 words / 10 marks)

### Q. Explain self-report inventory. Describe measures to avoid faking and methods to overcome weaknesses in self-report inventories. (10 marks)

*Also: "Discuss self-report personality tests with suitable examples." (2015-Jun, 2011-Dec); "Define and elucidate the various types of self-report inventories and describe any two of them." (2020-Dec)*

**Introduction**

Self-report inventories are structured personality assessment instruments in which respondents answer a series of standardised questions or statements about their own thoughts, feelings, and behaviours. Unlike projective techniques which rely on ambiguous stimuli, self-report inventories present clear items with defined response formats (true/false, Likert scales, forced-choice). They are the most widely used method of personality assessment due to their efficiency, standardisation, and psychometric properties.

**Types of Self-Report Inventories**

**1. Minnesota Multiphasic Personality Inventory (MMPI-2)**
Developed by Hathaway and McKinley (1943), revised as MMPI-2 (1989). Contains 567 true/false items across 10 clinical scales (Hypochondriasis, Depression, Hysteria, Psychopathic Deviance, Masculinity-Femininity, Paranoia, Psychasthenia, Schizophrenia, Hypomania, Social Introversion) plus validity scales (L, F, K). Widely used in clinical and forensic settings.

**2. NEO Personality Inventory-Revised (NEO-PI-R)**
Developed by Costa and McCrae (1992). 240 items measuring the Big Five factors (OCEAN) and 30 facets. Gold standard for normal personality assessment in research and applied contexts.

**3. Eysenck Personality Questionnaire (EPQ)**
Measures Eysenck's three dimensions (E, N, P) plus a Lie scale. Efficient 90-item measure with strong cross-cultural data.

**4. Cattell's 16PF Questionnaire**
Measures 16 primary source traits. Used in occupational selection and clinical assessment.

**5. California Psychological Inventory (CPI)**
Designed for use with normal, non-clinical populations. Measures 20 folk concepts (e.g., dominance, sociability, self-control).

**Strengths of Self-Report Inventories**

1. **Economical**: Can be administered to large groups simultaneously
2. **Standardised**: Uniform administration, scoring, and interpretation across respondents
3. **Quantifiable**: Provide numerical scores enabling statistical analysis and normative comparison
4. **Comprehensive**: Cover a wide range of personality dimensions
5. **Easy to administer**: Require minimal training compared to projective techniques
6. **Good reliability**: High test-retest and internal consistency

**Weaknesses and Limitations**

1. **Faking Good**: Deliberately presenting oneself in a favourable light (social desirability)
2. **Faking Bad**: Exaggerating symptoms or problems (malingering) — common in forensic settings
3. **Response Sets**: Acquiescence (tendency to agree) and deviation (tendency to choose unusual responses) regardless of content
4. **Limited Insight**: Respondents may lack self-awareness — report what they believe rather than how they actually are
5. **Cultural Bias**: Items may not translate well across cultures
6. **Cannot Access Unconscious**: Self-report is inherently limited to conscious awareness

**Methods to Overcome Weaknesses**

1. **Validity Scales**: MMPI-2 includes L (Lie), F (Infrequency), K (Defensiveness) scales to detect faking and random responding
2. **Forced-Choice Format**: Respondent must choose between two equally desirable (or undesirable) options — removes social desirability from the equation (e.g., Edwards Personal Preference Schedule)
3. **Subtle Items**: Including items whose connection to the construct is not obvious makes deliberate faking harder
4. **Warning Against Faking**: Informing respondents that detection of faking is possible reduces its occurrence
5. **Cross-validation**: Comparing self-report with observer ratings and behavioural data

**Conclusion**

Self-report inventories remain the most practical and psychometrically sound approach to large-scale personality assessment. Their standardisation and quantifiability make them invaluable in research and applied settings, though their limitations regarding faking and self-awareness require that they be complemented with other assessment methods for comprehensive evaluation.
