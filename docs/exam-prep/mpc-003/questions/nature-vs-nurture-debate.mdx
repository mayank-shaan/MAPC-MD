## 📝 Master Answer — Section A (450 words / 10 marks)

### Q. Discuss the issues of personality in the context of nature versus nurture. (10 marks)

*Also: "Discuss the nature and nurture debate with research examples." (2021-Dec B5); "Explain in detail the person-situation controversy." (2014-Jun)*

**Introduction**

One of the oldest questions in psychology is whether personality is determined primarily by biological inheritance (nature) or by environmental experience (nurture). The **nature vs nurture debate** has evolved from a simple dichotomy into a complex investigation of gene-environment interactions. In personality psychology, this debate addresses whether traits are genetically encoded or socially constructed — and the answer has profound implications for how we understand human behaviour.

**The Nature Position**

Proponents of the nature position argue that personality traits are largely genetically determined. Evidence includes:

1. **Twin Studies**: Identical twins (sharing 100% of genes) are significantly more similar in personality than fraternal twins (sharing ~50%). Bouchard et al.'s (1990) Minnesota Study of Twins Reared Apart found ~50% heritability for major personality traits, even for twins raised in different environments.

2. **Adoption Studies**: Adopted children's personality traits correlate more strongly with biological parents than adoptive parents, suggesting genetic transmission independent of shared environment.

3. **Infant Temperament**: Thomas and Chess identified stable temperamental differences in infants from the first weeks of life — differences that predict later personality. Three types: easy, difficult, and slow-to-warm-up.

4. **Heritability of the Big Five**: Each of the OCEAN factors shows moderate to high heritability (~40–60%).

**The Nurture Position**

Proponents of the nurture position argue that personality is shaped by experience:

1. **Behavioural Approaches**: Watson and Skinner demonstrated that behaviour can be systematically shaped through conditioning. Personality traits reflect cumulative reinforcement histories.

2. **Cross-cultural Variation**: The existence of culturally specific personality patterns (e.g., collectivist vs individualist self-concepts) demonstrates the power of socialisation.

3. **Family and Parenting Influence**: Baumrind's research on parenting styles (authoritative, authoritarian, permissive) shows consistent effects on children's personality and social competence.

4. **Life Events**: Major life experiences (trauma, education, migration) can produce lasting personality change.

**The Person-Situation Controversy**

Mischel (1968) challenged the trait concept itself, arguing that behaviour is more determined by situational variables than stable traits — correlations between measured traits and behaviour rarely exceed 0.30 (*personality coefficient*). This sparked the interactionist resolution: personality and situation interact to determine behaviour. Endler and Magnusson's interactionism argues that trait expressions depend on situational affordances and constraints.

**Interactionist Resolution**

Contemporary personality science embraces gene-environment interaction (G×E). The same genetic predisposition may express differently depending on the environment — diathesis-stress models, differential susceptibility theory (Belsky). **Epigenetics** shows that environmental experiences can modify gene expression without altering DNA sequences.

**Conclusion**

The nature vs nurture debate is now understood as a false dichotomy. Personality emerges from the continuous, bidirectional interaction between genetic predispositions and environmental experiences. Understanding this interaction is essential for both personality science and clinical intervention.

## 💡 Exam Tips

- Cover **both positions with evidence** before presenting the interactionist resolution.
- The **person-situation controversy** (Mischel's 0.30 correlation) is a key exam point — always mention it.
- Epigenetics and G×E interaction are contemporary points that demonstrate up-to-date knowledge.
