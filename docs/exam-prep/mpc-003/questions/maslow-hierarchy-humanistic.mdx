## 📝 Master Answer — Section A (450 words / 10 marks)

### Q. Discuss the humanistic theory of personality. (10 marks)

*Also: "Discuss the Humanistic approach of Personality Development. Differentiate between Maslow's and Roger's theory." (2014-Jun); "Describe the hierarchy of needs as proposed by Maslow." (2016-Jun, 2021-Dec)*

**Introduction**

Humanistic psychology emerged in the 1950s–60s as a "third force" in psychology, challenging both psychoanalysis (which focused on unconscious pathology) and behaviourism (which reduced persons to stimulus-response machines). Abraham Maslow and Carl Rogers are its leading figures. The humanistic approach emphasises human potential, free will, subjective experience, and the drive toward growth and self-actualisation.

**Maslow's Hierarchy of Needs**

Abraham Maslow (1908–1970) proposed that human motivation is organised hierarchically — lower-level needs must be substantially satisfied before higher-level needs become motivationally active. The hierarchy has five levels:

1. **Physiological Needs** (lowest): Biological requirements for survival — food, water, sleep, warmth, air. Without these, the body cannot function.

2. **Safety Needs**: Security, stability, freedom from fear, order, and protection from elements — physical safety, employment security, financial stability.

3. **Love and Belonging Needs**: Social connections — friendship, intimacy, family, sense of belonging and acceptance.

4. **Esteem Needs**: Two components:
   - *Self-esteem*: Achievement, mastery, independence, dignity
   - *Desire for reputation/prestige*: Status, dominance, and recognition from others

5. **Self-Actualisation** (highest): Realising one's full potential; becoming the best version of oneself. The specific form varies by individual ("What a man can be, he must be" — Maslow).

**Deficiency Needs vs Growth Needs**

- **Deficiency (D) Needs**: Levels 1–4. Motivated by a *lack* — they create a deficit drive that is satisfied when the need is met. Once satisfied, the motivation to pursue them diminishes.
- **Growth/Being (B) Needs**: Level 5 (self-actualisation). Motivated by the *desire to grow* — they are never permanently satisfied; meeting them increases rather than decreases motivation.

**Characteristics of Self-Actualisers**

Maslow studied healthy, high-functioning individuals (Lincoln, Einstein, Eleanor Roosevelt) and identified 15 characteristics of self-actualised people, including: realistic perception, acceptance of self and others, spontaneity, problem-centredness, need for privacy, autonomy and independence, continued freshness of appreciation, peak experiences, social interest, deep interpersonal relationships, democratic character, ethical discrimination, and a philosophical sense of humour.

**Peak Experiences**

Moments of profound happiness, harmony, and deep meaning — characterised by feelings of wholeness, effortlessness, and transcendence. Common in self-actualisers but not exclusive to them.

**Critique**

The hierarchical sequence is not universal — people in war zones may seek love over safety; artists may sacrifice physiological needs for self-expression. Maslow's sample was culturally limited and non-random. The concept of self-actualisation remains difficult to operationalise and measure empirically.

**Conclusion**

Maslow's hierarchy provides an optimistic, growth-oriented model of personality that has been enormously influential in counselling, organisational psychology, and education. By placing self-actualisation as the ultimate human striving, he shifted psychology's focus from pathology to human potential.

## 📝 Section B Answer (250 words / 6 marks)

### Q. Discuss deficiency and growth needs with reference to Maslow's Hierarchy of Needs. (6 marks)

*Asked directly in: 2024-Jun B9, 2018-Jun C, 2014-Dec*

Define the five-level hierarchy briefly → explain D-needs (levels 1–4, deficit motivation, diminish when satisfied) → explain B/G-needs (level 5, growth motivation, increase when pursued) → give one example for each → note that D-needs must be met before B-needs become salient. ~250 words.
