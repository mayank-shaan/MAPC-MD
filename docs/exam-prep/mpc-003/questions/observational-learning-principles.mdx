## 📝 Master Answer — Section B (250 words / 6 marks)

### Q. Discuss in detail the principles of observational learning as stated by Albert Bandura. (6 marks)

*Also: "Discuss the steps involved in the modelling process." (2022-Dec B8)*

**Observational Learning (Modelling)**

Observational learning — learning by watching others — is Bandura's most influential concept. Unlike classical and operant conditioning, it does not require direct reinforcement. Four sub-processes govern whether observed behaviour is acquired and performed:

**1. Attentional Processes**
The observer must attend to the model's behaviour. Attention is influenced by:
- Model's perceived prestige, attractiveness, and similarity to observer
- Observer's arousal, expectancy, and sensory capacities
- Distinctiveness and complexity of the modelled behaviour

**2. Retention Processes**
Observed behaviour must be encoded into memory as symbolic representations — verbal codes (verbal description) or imaginal codes (mental images). Cognitive rehearsal strengthens retention.

**3. Motor Reproduction Processes**
The observer must have the physical and cognitive capability to reproduce the behaviour. Sub-skills must already exist; motor reproduction involves self-observation and corrective feedback.

**4. Motivational Processes**
Acquisition does not guarantee performance. The observer performs the behaviour only when sufficiently motivated. Three sources of motivation:
- *Direct reinforcement*: Observer is directly rewarded for imitating
- *Vicarious reinforcement*: Observing the model being rewarded increases imitation
- *Self-reinforcement*: Meeting personal standards provides intrinsic motivation

**Factors Affecting Modelling**

Modelling is more likely when the model is: similar to the observer, of higher status or expertise, and seen as warm and nurturing. Children are more susceptible to modelling than adults.

## 💡 Exam Tips
- Always list and explain **all four sub-processes** — each is worth marks.
- Distinguish between *learning* (acquisition) and *performance* — motivational processes explain why learned behaviours are not always performed.
