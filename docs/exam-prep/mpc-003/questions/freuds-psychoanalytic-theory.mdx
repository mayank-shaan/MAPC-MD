## 📝 Master Answer — Section A (450 words / 10 marks)

### Q. Explain Sigmund Freud's Psychoanalytic theory of personality. (10 marks)

*Also: "Discuss the stages of psychosexual development proposed by Freud." (2017-Dec, 2021-Jun); "Explain Freud's structural model of personality and give examples of any two defense mechanisms." (2024-Dec B6)*

**Introduction**

Sigmund Freud (1856–1939) founded psychoanalysis and developed the first comprehensive theory of personality based on clinical observation. His theory rests on three central propositions: (1) unconscious mental processes powerfully influence behaviour, (2) personality is rooted in early childhood experiences, and (3) psychic energy (libido) drives all mental activity. Freud's theory encompasses a topographic model, a structural model, psychosexual stages, and defense mechanisms.

**Topographic Model**

Freud initially described the mind in terms of three levels of consciousness:
- **Conscious**: What we are currently aware of
- **Preconscious**: Material not currently conscious but accessible with effort (memories, stored knowledge)
- **Unconscious**: Repressed wishes, fears, conflicts, and impulses — not directly accessible but powerfully influencing behaviour. The largest and most important layer.

**Structural Model (Id, Ego, Superego)**

Freud later replaced the topographic model with the structural model:
- **Id**: Entirely unconscious; operates on the *pleasure principle* — seeks immediate gratification of instinctual drives (libido and aggression); primary process thinking (wish fulfilment, illogical)
- **Ego**: Partly conscious; operates on the *reality principle* — mediates between id demands and external reality; secondary process thinking (logical, rational). The executive of the personality.
- **Superego**: Partly conscious; the internalised moral standards derived from parental and societal prohibitions. Contains the **conscience** (punishment for violations) and the **ego ideal** (standards for perfection).

Anxiety arises when id impulses threaten to overwhelm the ego, triggering defense mechanisms.

**Psychosexual Stages of Development**

Freud proposed that personality develops through five stages, each centred on a different erogenous zone. Unresolved conflicts at any stage lead to **fixation** — the persistence of stage-related characteristics into adulthood:

| Stage | Age | Focus | Fixation Consequences |
|-------|-----|-------|----------------------|
| Oral | 0–18 months | Mouth (feeding) | Dependency, pessimism, sarcasm, smoking |
| Anal | 18 months–3 years | Anal region (toilet training) | Anality: orderliness, miserliness, obstinacy (retentive) or messiness, impulsiveness (expulsive) |
| Phallic | 3–6 years | Genitals; Oedipus/Electra complex | Vanity, recklessness, sexual difficulties |
| Latency | 6–puberty | Sexual impulses dormant; social skills develop | — |
| Genital | Puberty onwards | Mature heterosexual relationships | Healthy adult personality if no prior fixations |

**Defense Mechanisms**

The ego uses defense mechanisms to manage anxiety by distorting reality:
1. **Repression**: Banishing threatening thoughts/memories to the unconscious
2. **Projection**: Attributing one's own unacceptable impulses to others
3. **Rationalisation**: Providing logical justifications for unacceptable behaviour
4. **Reaction Formation**: Expressing the opposite of one's true feelings
5. **Displacement**: Redirecting impulses from the threatening target to a safer one
6. **Sublimation**: Channelling unacceptable impulses into socially acceptable activities (the healthiest mechanism)
7. **Regression**: Reverting to behaviours of an earlier developmental stage under stress
8. **Denial**: Refusing to accept a threatening reality

**Conclusion**

Freud's psychoanalytic theory, despite its limitations (non-falsifiability, male-centric bias, cultural narrowness), fundamentally transformed our understanding of personality by highlighting the role of the unconscious, early experience, and intrapsychic conflict. Its concepts remain influential in clinical psychology, literary analysis, and cultural theory.
