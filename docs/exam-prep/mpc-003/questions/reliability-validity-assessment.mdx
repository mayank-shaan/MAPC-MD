## 📝 Master Answer — Section B (250 words / 6 marks)

### Q. Elucidate the concepts of reliability and validity. (6 marks)

*Also: "Define Reliability and Validity as measurement concepts." (2021-Jun B9); "Discuss the concept of validity and its relevance in personality testing." (2014-Jun)*

**Reliability**

Reliability refers to the **consistency** of a measurement instrument — the extent to which it produces the same results under the same conditions. A reliable test is free from random measurement error.

**Types of Reliability:**

1. *Test-retest reliability*: Administering the same test to the same group on two occasions and correlating the scores. Indicates temporal stability.
2. *Split-half reliability*: Splitting test items into two halves and correlating the scores. Measures internal consistency.
3. *Inter-rater reliability*: Degree of agreement between two or more scorers. Critical for projective techniques and behavioural observation.
4. *Parallel forms reliability*: Correlation between two equivalent versions of the test. Eliminates practice effects.

Reliability is expressed as a correlation coefficient (0–1). A coefficient above 0.80 is generally acceptable for personality measures.

**Validity**

Validity refers to the **accuracy** of a measurement instrument — whether it measures what it is intended to measure. Validity is always specific to a purpose.

**Types of Validity:**

1. *Content validity*: Items represent the full domain of the construct (judged by expert review)
2. *Criterion validity*:
   - *Predictive*: Scores predict a future criterion (e.g., EPQ predicts antisocial behaviour)
   - *Concurrent*: Scores correlate with a current criterion measure
3. *Construct validity*: The test measures the theoretical construct — demonstrated through convergent validity (correlates with related measures) and discriminant validity (does not correlate with unrelated measures)
4. *Face validity*: The test appears to measure what it should — important for respondent acceptance

**Reliability vs Validity**

A test can be reliable without being valid (consistent but measuring the wrong thing), but a valid test must be reliable. Reliability is a necessary but not sufficient condition for validity.
