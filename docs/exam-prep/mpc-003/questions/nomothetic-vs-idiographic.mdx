## 📝 Master Answer — Section B (250 words / 6 marks)

### Q. Differentiate between nomothetic and idiographic approaches to personality. (6 marks)

*Also: "Highlight the nomothetic versus ideographic debate in personality." (2014-Dec); "Explain nomothetic approach vs idiographic approach to personality." (2018-Jun B5)*

**Nomothetic Approach**

The nomothetic approach (from Greek *nomos* = law) aims to identify **general laws and principles** that apply to all people. It focuses on dimensions of personality shared across individuals, allowing comparison and statistical analysis. Examples:
- Eysenck's PEN model
- Cattell's 16PF
- The Five Factor Model
- Standardised personality inventories

*Characteristics*: Quantitative, statistical, group-based, uses factor analysis, seeks universal dimensions, values prediction and control.

**Idiographic Approach**

The idiographic approach (from Greek *idios* = private) focuses on the **uniqueness of the individual**, seeking to understand the particular person in their full complexity. It resists reducing personality to scores on universal dimensions. Examples:
- Allport's personal dispositions (cardinal, central, secondary)
- Case study method
- Narrative approaches to personality

*Characteristics*: Qualitative, in-depth, individual-focused, values understanding over prediction, uses life histories and biographical methods.

**Key Differences**

| Dimension | Nomothetic | Idiographic |
|-----------|-----------|-------------|
| Goal | Universal laws | Individual understanding |
| Method | Quantitative, statistical | Qualitative, case-based |
| Unit of study | Group/population | Individual |
| Theorists | Eysenck, Cattell | Allport |
| Strength | Allows comparison; testable | Rich, holistic understanding |
| Limitation | Misses individual uniqueness | Cannot generalise |

**Resolution**

Allport argued that both approaches are necessary — nomothetic methods establish the dimensional framework; idiographic methods describe individual profiles within it. Contemporary personality psychology uses **person-centred analysis** (cluster analysis, Q-methodology) to bridge both approaches.
