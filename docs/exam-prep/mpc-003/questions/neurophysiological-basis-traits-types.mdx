## 📝 Master Answer — Section A (450 words / 10 marks)

### Q. Discuss in detail the neurophysiological basis of traits and types. (10 marks)

*Also: "Explain the neurophysiological basis of traits and types." (2024-Jun B5, 2022-Dec A3); "Discuss the neuropsychological basis of traits and types." (2016-Dec A3)*

**Introduction**

Eysenck's theory of personality is distinctive in providing a neurophysiological explanation for the major personality dimensions — not merely describing them statistically but accounting for them in terms of brain structures and functions. He proposed that individual differences in personality dimensions (Extraversion, Neuroticism, Psychoticism) reflect underlying differences in the nervous system.

**Neurophysiological Basis of Extraversion–Introversion**

The key mechanism is **cortical arousal**, mediated by the **Ascending Reticular Activating System (ARAS)** — a neural network in the brainstem that regulates the overall activation level of the cerebral cortex.

- **Introverts** have a chronically **higher baseline level of cortical arousal** (their ARAS generates stronger excitatory signals). Because they are already highly aroused, they seek to reduce stimulation — preferring quiet, solitary activities, and avoiding intense social environments. Introversion is thus a self-regulation strategy to manage high arousal.

- **Extraverts** have a chronically **lower baseline cortical arousal**. They are chronically under-stimulated and therefore seek external stimulation — social interaction, excitement, novelty, and risk — to raise arousal to an optimal level.

**Empirical Support**: Introverts show greater arousal on EEG measures, respond more strongly to caffeine (a stimulant), and have lower sensory thresholds. They also condition more easily (pavlovian conditioning) due to their higher arousal.

**Neurophysiological Basis of Neuroticism–Emotional Stability**

Neuroticism is linked to the **visceral brain** — primarily the **limbic system** — which governs emotional reactivity:

- Individuals high on Neuroticism have a **more reactive limbic system**: stronger, faster emotional responses to stimuli and slower return to baseline after emotional activation.
- Individuals low on Neuroticism (emotionally stable) have a less reactive limbic system: moderate emotional responses that return quickly to baseline.

The limbic system structures involved include: the **hippocampus** (threat detection), **amygdala** (fear and emotional memory), **hypothalamus** (autonomic regulation), and **cingulate gyrus**.

**Neurophysiological Basis of Psychoticism**

Psychoticism is less clearly mapped but is associated with:
- **Testosterone levels**: Higher testosterone correlates with aggression, impulsivity, and callousness
- **Dopaminergic activity**: High dopamine is linked to sensation-seeking and disinhibited behaviour
- **Serotonin deficiency**: Associated with impulsivity and aggression

**Integration: Conditionability and Personality Disorder**

Eysenck argued that the neurophysiological bases have important implications for conditionability — the ease with which individuals form conditioned responses:
- High arousal (introverts) → easy conditionability → rapid socialisation → conforming, anxious, neurotic behaviour
- Low arousal (extraverts) → poor conditionability → inadequate socialisation → risk of antisocial/psychopathic behaviour when combined with high Psychoticism

**Conclusion**

Eysenck's neurophysiological model represents a landmark integration of personality theory with neuroscience. By grounding personality dimensions in brain structures and functions, it provided testable predictions and established the template for contemporary biological approaches to personality.
