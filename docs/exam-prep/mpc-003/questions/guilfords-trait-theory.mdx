## 📝 Master Answer — Section A (450 words / 10 marks)

### Q. Explain Guilford's trait theory of personality. (10 marks)

*Also: "Compare between Type and Trait approaches to personality. Describe Guilford's trait theory." (2021-Jun)*

**Introduction**

J.P. Guilford (1897–1987), an American psychologist, developed a comprehensive trait theory of personality using factor analysis. He defined personality as "a person's unique pattern of traits," and a trait as "any distinguishable, relatively enduring way in which one individual differs from others." Guilford's approach, like Cattell's, was nomothetic and statistical — aiming to identify the fundamental dimensions of personality through systematic measurement.

**Guilford's Seven Modalities of Traits**

Guilford classified personality traits into seven broad areas, encompassing the totality of individual differences:

1. **Morphological Traits (Somatic)**: Physical characteristics — body build, height, weight, facial features. These biological characteristics influence personality through their effects on self-image and social reactions.

2. **Physiological Traits**: Biochemical and neurological characteristics — hormonal levels, autonomic reactivity, metabolic rate. Eysenck's cortical arousal theory is an example of physiological trait research.

3. **Aptitude/Ability Traits**: Cognitive capacities — intelligence, creativity, special aptitudes. Guilford made significant contributions to intelligence theory, proposing the Structure of Intellect model (120+ factors).

4. **Temperament Traits**: Emotional and stylistic aspects of behaviour — activity level, emotional reactivity, subjective well-being. These are constitutional and relatively stable.

5. **Needs/Motivational Traits**: Internal drives, motives, and goal-directed tendencies. Guilford recognised both biological drives and acquired motives.

6. **Interest Traits**: Preferences for particular activities, environments, or objects. Vocational interests (Holland's RIASEC types, for example) represent interest traits.

7. **Attitudes**: Evaluative dispositions toward objects, people, or situations. Attitudes combine cognitive, affective, and behavioural components.

**Guilford's Factor-Analytic Research**

Through extensive factor analysis of personality questionnaire data, Guilford identified a set of temperament traits forming the basis of the **Guilford-Zimmerman Temperament Survey (GZTS)**, which measures ten dimensions including: General Activity, Restraint, Ascendance, Sociability, Emotional Stability, Objectivity, Friendliness, Thoughtfulness, Personal Relations, and Masculinity.

**Comparison with Cattell and Eysenck**

| Feature | Guilford | Cattell | Eysenck |
|---------|---------|---------|---------|
| Method | Factor analysis | Factor analysis | Factor analysis |
| Breadth | 7 modalities, very broad | Source/surface distinction | 3 super-factors |
| Measurement | GZTS | 16PF | EPQ |
| Emphasis | Comprehensive taxonomy | Data sources (L, Q, T) | Biological basis |

**Conclusion**

Guilford's trait theory provides one of the most comprehensive frameworks for understanding individual differences, encompassing biological, cognitive, motivational, and social dimensions of personality. While his model is broader and more complex than Eysenck's or Cattell's, this breadth also makes it less parsimonious and harder to apply practically.
