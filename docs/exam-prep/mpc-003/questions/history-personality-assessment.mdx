## 📝 Answer — Section B (250 words / 6 marks)

### Q. Trace the historical development of personality assessment. (6 marks)

*Also: "Discuss the history of personality assessment." (2012-Dec); "Trace the historical development of personality assessment." (2023-Jun A4)*

**Historical Development of Personality Assessment**

The history of personality assessment spans over 2,000 years, evolving from speculative philosophy to rigorous psychometrics.

**Ancient Period**: The earliest personality assessment systems were physiognomic — inferring character from physical features. Hippocrates' four humours (400 BC) linked bodily fluids to temperament types. Phrenology (Gall, early 19th century) attempted to map personality from skull contours — later discredited but influential in stimulating systematic study.

**Early Scientific Period (19th–early 20th century)**: Galton (1884) pioneered quantitative measurement of individual differences. Kraepelin introduced psychiatric classification, providing a framework for assessing personality pathology. Graphology (handwriting analysis) and physiognomy remained popular despite lacking validity.

**Psychoanalytic Era (1900s–1930s)**: Freud's clinical methods — free association, dream analysis, projective interpretation — shifted focus to unconscious processes. This inspired the projective testing movement. Murray and Morgan developed the TAT (1935); Rorschach published his inkblot test (1921).

**Psychometric Era (1920s–1950s)**: World War I accelerated development of group intelligence and personality tests. Woodworth's Personal Data Sheet (1917) — first standardised personality questionnaire — assessed neurotic symptoms in soldiers. The MMPI (1943) established the empirical keying approach. Factor analysis (Cattell, Eysenck) generated trait-based inventories.

**Contemporary Period**: Development of the NEO-PI (1985), Five Factor Model consensus, computer-based adaptive testing, neuroimaging correlates of personality, and evidence-based assessment standards.
