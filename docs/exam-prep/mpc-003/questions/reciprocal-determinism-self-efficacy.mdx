## 📝 Master Answer — Section C (100 words / 3 marks)

### Q. Reciprocal Determinism (short note). (3 marks)

**Reciprocal Determinism** is Albert Bandura's model of the triadic interaction between **Person** (cognitive factors, beliefs, expectations), **Behaviour** (actions taken), and **Environment** (external stimuli and reinforcement). These three determinants mutually influence each other in a continuous, bidirectional cycle. Unlike Skinner's one-directional environmental determinism, Bandura's model grants the person an active, agentic role. For example, a student's self-efficacy beliefs (person) affect their study behaviour (behaviour), which shapes their academic environment through grades and teacher feedback (environment), which in turn reinforces or modifies self-efficacy.

## 📝 Section B Answer (250 words / 6 marks)

### Q. Describe the concept of self-system. Explain the sources and role of self-efficacy. (6 marks)

*Also: "Explain the sources and role of self-efficacy." (2020-Jun B6)*

**Self-System**

Bandura's **self-system** refers to the internal cognitive structures through which individuals observe, evaluate, and regulate their own behaviour. It operates via three processes: self-observation (monitoring one's behaviour), judgmental process (evaluating against personal standards), and self-response (self-reinforcement or self-punishment based on evaluation). The self-system makes humans self-directing agents rather than passive products of external reinforcement.

**Self-Efficacy**

**Self-efficacy** is the individual's belief in their own capacity to execute the behaviours required to produce specific outcomes. It is situation-specific (not a global trait) and distinct from self-esteem (which is a global evaluation of worth).

**Four Sources of Self-Efficacy:**

1. **Mastery Experiences**: Direct personal success is the most powerful source. Past success raises self-efficacy; repeated failure lowers it.

2. **Vicarious Experiences (Modelling)**: Observing similar others succeed ("if they can do it, so can I"). Especially powerful when the model is perceived as similar in ability.

3. **Verbal Persuasion (Social Encouragement)**: Encouragement from trusted others (parents, teachers, coaches) raises self-efficacy. Criticism lowers it.

4. **Physiological and Affective States**: People interpret their own arousal as information about competence. Anxiety is often read as low efficacy; calm and energised states support high efficacy.

**Role of Self-Efficacy**

High self-efficacy → approach tasks, set challenging goals, persist under adversity, recover from setbacks.  
Low self-efficacy → avoidance, low aspirations, rapid disengagement, anxiety.

Self-efficacy is the primary mechanism of change in cognitive-behavioural therapy (Bandura, 1977).
