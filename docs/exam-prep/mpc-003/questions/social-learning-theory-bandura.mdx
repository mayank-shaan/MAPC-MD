## 📝 Master Answer — Section A (450 words / 10 marks)

### Q. Describe Albert Bandura's Social Learning Theory. (10 marks)

*Appeared in: 2011-Jun, 2011-Dec, 2012-Dec, 2013-Dec, 2015-Dec, 2016-Dec, 2017-Jun, 2019-Dec, 2021-Jun, 2021-Dec, 2023-Jun*

**Introduction**

Albert Bandura's Social Learning Theory, developed in the 1960s and later expanded into Social Cognitive Theory, bridges behaviourism and cognitive psychology. Bandura argued that personality and behaviour are shaped not just by direct reinforcement (as Skinner proposed) but also by observation, modelling, and cognitive processes such as self-efficacy and self-regulation. His theory emphasises that humans are active agents who influence and are influenced by their environments.

**Observational Learning (Modelling)**

Bandura's most significant contribution is the concept of **observational learning** — the ability to learn by watching others, without direct reinforcement. The modelling process involves four sub-processes:

1. **Attentional Processes**: The observer must attend to the model's behaviour. Attention is influenced by the model's attractiveness, status, similarity to the observer, and the observer's arousal level.
2. **Retention Processes**: Observed behaviour must be encoded and stored in memory as symbolic representations (verbal or imaginal) for later retrieval.
3. **Motor Reproduction Processes**: The observer must have the physical capability to reproduce the modelled behaviour. Mental rehearsal aids this process.
4. **Motivational Processes**: The observer reproduces behaviour only when sufficiently motivated. Motivation can come from direct reinforcement, vicarious reinforcement (seeing the model rewarded), or self-reinforcement.

**Reciprocal Determinism**

Bandura proposed that personality is shaped by **reciprocal determinism** — a triadic interaction among:
- **Person** (cognitive factors, beliefs, expectations)
- **Behaviour** (actions taken)
- **Environment** (external stimuli and consequences)

These three determinants continuously influence each other, creating a dynamic, bidirectional system. Unlike Skinner's one-way environmental determinism, Bandura's model grants the person an active, agentic role.

**Self-Efficacy**

A central construct in Bandura's theory is **self-efficacy** — the individual's belief in their own capacity to execute behaviours required to produce specific outcomes. It is not about one's skills per se, but about one's confidence in using those skills. Sources of self-efficacy include:
1. *Mastery experiences*: Direct personal success
2. *Vicarious experiences*: Observing similar others succeed
3. *Verbal persuasion*: Encouragement from others
4. *Physiological states*: Interpreting arousal as competence or anxiety

Self-efficacy shapes whether people attempt tasks, how long they persist, and how they react to failure.

**Self-System and Self-Regulation**

Bandura also described an internal **self-system** — the set of cognitive structures and processes by which individuals observe their own behaviour, judge it against personal standards, and regulate it through self-reinforcement and self-punishment. This makes humans fundamentally self-directing agents rather than passive recipients of environmental contingencies.

**Conclusion**

Bandura's Social Learning Theory offers a comprehensive account of personality development that integrates learning principles with cognitive processes. Its emphasis on self-efficacy, reciprocal determinism, and observational learning has had profound implications for education, therapy (cognitive-behavioural approaches), and health psychology. It remains one of the most empirically supported theories of personality and behaviour.

## 📝 Section B Answer (250 words / 6 marks)

### Q. Discuss the self-system with reference to Bandura's theory of social learning. (6 marks)

*Also asked as: "Describe the concept of self-system. Explain the sources and role of self-efficacy." (2020-Jun)*

**Self-System in Bandura's Theory**

Bandura's concept of the **self-system** refers to the internal cognitive structures that enable individuals to observe, evaluate, and regulate their own behaviour — making humans active agents rather than passive products of their environment.

The self-system operates through three key processes:

**1. Self-Observation**: Individuals monitor their own behaviour, paying attention to its dimensions (quality, rate, quantity) and the contexts in which it occurs. This provides the information needed for self-evaluation.

**2. Judgmental Process**: Observed behaviour is evaluated against personal standards (derived from social norms, prior performance, and modelling). This determines whether the individual feels pride, satisfaction, or dissatisfaction.

**3. Self-Response**: Based on the evaluation, the individual engages in self-reinforcement (rewarding oneself for meeting standards) or self-punishment (criticising oneself for falling short). These internal responses sustain behaviour independently of external rewards.

**Role of Self-Efficacy**

Self-efficacy — the belief in one's competence — is the cornerstone of the self-system. High self-efficacy leads to approach behaviour, persistence, and resilience in the face of failure. Low self-efficacy leads to avoidance, anxiety, and rapid disengagement. Self-efficacy is situation-specific: a person may have high efficacy for academic tasks but low efficacy for social interactions.

In therapeutic contexts, enhancing self-efficacy through mastery experiences and guided participation is a primary mechanism of change in cognitive-behavioural interventions.
