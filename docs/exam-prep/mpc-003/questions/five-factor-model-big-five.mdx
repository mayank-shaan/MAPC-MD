## 📝 Master Answer — Section A (450 words / 10 marks)

### Q. Define personality. Explain the Five Factor Model (Big Five) of personality. (10 marks)

*Also: "Describe and elucidate the theoretical principles of the Big Five theory. Explain the measurement of the Big Five Inventory." (2021-Jun); "Describe the Five Factor Model." (2012-Dec, 2016-Jun)*

**Introduction**

The Five Factor Model (FFM), also called the Big Five, is the most empirically supported descriptive taxonomy of human personality. It emerged from the lexical hypothesis — the idea that important personality characteristics are encoded in natural language — and decades of factor-analytic research. Allport and Odbert (1936) identified ~18,000 personality-descriptive adjectives in English; Cattell reduced these to 35 clusters; Fiske (1949) factor-analysed them and found five broad factors; Tupes and Christal (1961) confirmed these; and Costa and McCrae (1985, 1992) refined the model into the NEO-PI.

**The Five Factors (OCEAN)**

**1. Openness to Experience (O)**
Reflects intellectual curiosity, aesthetic sensitivity, imagination, and preference for novelty.
- *High scorers*: Creative, intellectually curious, open to new ideas, imaginative, unconventional
- *Low scorers*: Conventional, prefer routine, concrete, practical

**2. Conscientiousness (C)**
Reflects self-discipline, organisation, goal-directed behaviour, and reliability.
- *High scorers*: Organised, dependable, self-disciplined, achievement-oriented, planful
- *Low scorers*: Disorganised, careless, easily distracted, impulsive

**3. Extraversion (E)**
Reflects sociability, positive emotionality, assertiveness, and activity level.
- *High scorers*: Sociable, talkative, assertive, optimistic, energetic
- *Low scorers*: Reserved, independent, quiet, prefer solitude

**4. Agreeableness (A)**
Reflects altruism, trust, cooperation, and concern for others' welfare.
- *High scorers*: Cooperative, trusting, empathic, good-natured, helpful
- *Low scorers*: Competitive, suspicious, antagonistic, critical

**5. Neuroticism (N)**
Reflects emotional instability, anxiety proneness, and negative affectivity.
- *High scorers*: Anxious, moody, emotionally reactive, prone to stress and negative emotions
- *Low scorers*: Emotionally stable, calm, resilient, even-tempered

**Measurement**

The primary measurement tools are:
- **NEO-PI-R** (Costa & McCrae, 1992): 240-item questionnaire measuring all five factors plus six facets per factor (30 facets total). Gold standard for research and clinical use.
- **NEO-FFI**: Shortened 60-item version for practical use
- **Big Five Inventory (BFI)** (John & Srivastava, 1999): 44-item brief measure used widely in research

**Empirical Support and Critique**

The Big Five has been replicated across over 50 languages and cultures, showing remarkable cross-cultural consistency (McCrae & Costa, 1997). Twin studies indicate ~40–60% heritability for each factor. The model predicts important life outcomes: Conscientiousness predicts academic and occupational success; Neuroticism predicts psychological distress; Extraversion predicts life satisfaction.

Critiques: The model is *descriptive*, not explanatory — it describes the structure of personality but does not explain why traits develop or the mechanisms involved. Some argue six factors are needed (HEXACO model); others propose fewer. The model also does not adequately capture positive vs negative valence of traits.

**Conclusion**

The Five Factor Model represents the dominant empirical consensus on the structure of personality. Its cross-cultural replication, predictive validity, and heritable nature make it the most robust framework for personality description available. Measurement through the NEO-PI-R allows reliable, standardised assessment across clinical and research contexts.
