## 🌟 Comprehensive Answer: Factor Theories of Intelligence (~500 words)

### Introduction

Factor theories of intelligence emerged from attempts to **identify the basic dimensions** of human intelligence through statistical analysis. While **Spearman** proposed a single general intelligence (g), later theorists argued that intelligence is composed of **multiple independent or hierarchically organised factors**. The primary contributors to this tradition are **Louis Thurstone, Raymond Cattell**, and **Philip Vernon**.

---

### 1. Thurstone's Primary Mental Abilities (1938)

**Louis Thurstone** used **factor analysis** on a large battery of cognitive tests and identified **7 Primary Mental Abilities (PMA)**:

| Ability | Abbreviation | Description |
|---------|-------------|-------------|
| Verbal Comprehension | V | Understanding words and meanings |
| Word Fluency | W | Speed in producing words |
| Number | N | Arithmetical computation speed |
| Space | S | Visualizing objects in space |
| Associative Memory | M | Rote memory |
| Perceptual Speed | P | Quick identification of visual patterns |
| Reasoning/Induction | I/R | Identifying rules and relationships |

**Key claim**: These abilities are **relatively independent** — a person can be high in one and low in another. This directly challenged Spearman's unitary g.

**Later reconciliation**: Thurstone acknowledged a higher-order factor across his PMAs, which resembled Spearman's g — suggesting g and PMAs operate at different levels of analysis.

---

### 2. Cattell's Fluid vs Crystallised Intelligence (1963)

**Raymond Cattell** distinguished two forms of intelligence:

| Type | Definition | Brain Basis | Age Effect | Example |
|------|-----------|-------------|-----------|---------|
| **Fluid Intelligence (Gf)** | Raw reasoning ability, novel problem solving | Neurological integrity | Peaks early, declines with age | Logic puzzles, matrix reasoning |
| **Crystallised Intelligence (Gc)** | Accumulated knowledge and skills | Experience and culture | Stable or increases with age | Vocabulary, general knowledge |

Cattell argued that **Gf is biologically based** while **Gc is culturally shaped** through education and experience. This distinction has been highly influential in understanding cognitive aging — people typically retain Gc even as Gf declines.

**Horn (1965)** extended this into the Cattell-Horn model with additional factors (short-term memory, processing speed, visual processing).

---

### 3. Vernon's Hierarchical Model (1950)

**Philip Vernon** proposed a **hierarchical organisation** of intelligence:

```
g (General Factor)
├── v:ed (Verbal-Educational)
│   ├── Verbal ability
│   └── Numerical ability
└── k:m (Spatial-Mechanical)
    ├── Spatial ability
    └── Mechanical ability
```

This model reconciled Spearman and Thurstone: **g operates at the top**, with major group factors below, followed by minor group factors and specific abilities at the base.

---

### Evaluation

**Strengths:**
- Identified specific cognitive abilities with practical value (educational assessment, vocational guidance)
- Cattell's Gf/Gc distinction has strong empirical support and practical utility
- Hierarchical models offer a comprehensive structure

**Criticisms:**
- Factor analysis depends heavily on the tests included — different batteries yield different factors
- Debate over whether factors reflect genuine psychological entities or statistical artefacts
- Limited cross-cultural validity

---

### Conclusion

Factor theories transformed the study of intelligence from a search for a single general ability to a **multi-dimensional model**. Thurstone, Cattell, and Vernon collectively established that intelligence has both specific components and a unifying structure. Their work laid the foundation for modern intelligence testing and cognitive profiling, and Cattell's Gf/Gc distinction remains clinically and educationally significant.
