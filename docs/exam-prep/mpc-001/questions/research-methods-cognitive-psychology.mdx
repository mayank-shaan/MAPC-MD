## 🌟 Comprehensive Answer: Research Methods (~500 words)

### Introduction

Cognitive psychology studies internal mental processes — perception, attention, memory, language, problem solving, and decision making. Since these processes are not directly observable, researchers have developed a range of **specialised research methods** to infer mental activity from behaviour. These methods range from controlled laboratory experiments to neuroimaging and computational modelling.

---

### 1. Experimental Method

The **laboratory experiment** is the cornerstone of cognitive research. Participants respond to controlled stimuli, and researchers measure:

- **Reaction time (RT)**: Mental chronometry — time taken to process information reflects the complexity of underlying operations (Donders, 1868)
- **Error rates**: Types and frequency of errors reveal processing strategies
- **Dual-task paradigm**: Two tasks performed simultaneously reveal attentional limits and resource allocation

**Advantage**: High internal validity, causal conclusions possible  
**Limitation**: Low ecological validity — lab tasks may not reflect real-world cognition

---

### 2. Introspection

Pioneered by **Wilhelm Wundt** (Leipzig, 1879), participants were trained to observe and report their own mental states. Wundt called this **experimental introspection** or self-observation.

**Limitation**: Highly subjective, unreliable, and unable to access unconscious processing. Largely abandoned after behaviourist critique.

---

### 3. Protocol Analysis (Think-Aloud Method)

Participants **verbalise their thoughts** while performing a task. **Ericsson and Simon (1980)** formalised this as a valid research tool. Used extensively in problem-solving research (Newell & Simon).

**Advantage**: Reveals moment-by-moment mental processes  
**Limitation**: Verbalisation may interfere with or alter the actual cognitive process

---

### 4. Neuroimaging Methods

Modern cognitive psychology relies heavily on brain imaging:

| Method | What It Measures | Temporal Resolution |
|--------|-----------------|---------------------|
| **fMRI** | Blood oxygen levels (neural activity) | Seconds |
| **PET** | Cerebral blood flow | Minutes |
| **EEG/ERP** | Electrical brain activity | Milliseconds |
| **TMS** | Disrupts specific brain regions | Real-time |

These methods allow researchers to identify **which brain regions** are active during specific cognitive tasks.

---

### 5. Case Study / Neuropsychological Method

Studying individuals with **brain damage or neurological disorders** reveals the function of specific brain structures. Classic cases:
- **H.M. (Henry Molaison)** — bilateral hippocampectomy revealed hippocampus's role in memory consolidation
- **Phineas Gage** — frontal lobe damage revealed frontal lobe's role in personality and decision-making
- **Broca's patient "Tan"** — revealed Broca's area and language production

---

### 6. Computational Modelling

Researchers build **computer simulations** of cognitive processes to test theories. If the model replicates human performance, it validates the theory. Used extensively in memory (connectionist PDP models) and problem solving (General Problem Solver — Newell & Simon).

---

### Evaluation

Each method has strengths and weaknesses. **Triangulation** — using multiple methods — increases confidence in findings. Modern cognitive neuroscience combines behavioural experiments with neuroimaging for comprehensive understanding.

---

### Conclusion

Research methods in cognitive psychology have evolved from simple introspection to sophisticated neuroimaging and computational modelling. Each method provides a different window into mental processes, and the convergence of findings across methods has built robust theories of cognition. The choice of method depends on the research question — whether the goal is to identify the **timing** of processes, their **neural substrate**, or their **functional organisation**.
