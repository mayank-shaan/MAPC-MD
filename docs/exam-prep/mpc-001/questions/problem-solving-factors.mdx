## 🌟 Comprehensive Answer: Factors Affecting Problem Solving (~500 words)

### Introduction

Problem solving is a **complex cognitive process** influenced by numerous interacting factors. The same problem may be solved easily by one person and be insurmountable for another, depending on their knowledge, mindset, motivation, and environmental context. Understanding the factors that facilitate or hinder problem solving is essential for improving performance in academic, professional, and everyday settings.

---

### 1. Problem Representation

**How a problem is initially represented** (mentally framed) profoundly affects solution success. A poor representation can lead the solver away from the solution; restructuring the problem often leads to breakthroughs.

- **Duncker (1945)**: Participants were less likely to use a box as a candle platform when it was presented as a container (functional fixedness) — the representation of the box determined usability
- **Insight solutions** typically involve **restructuring** the problem representation (Gestalt perspective)

---

### 2. Mental Set (Einstellung Effect)

A **mental set** is a predisposition to approach a problem in a particular way based on past experience — even when that approach is suboptimal.

**Luchins' Water Jar Experiments (1942):** Participants trained to solve water jar problems using a complex method continued using that method even when a simpler solution was obvious. They were **set** by prior experience.

- Mental set can be **helpful** (when past strategies apply) or **harmful** (when they blind the solver to better solutions)

---

### 3. Functional Fixedness

**Duncker (1945)** demonstrated that people tend to see objects only in terms of their **conventional use**, preventing creative problem solving.

Classic experiment: Participants given a candle, box of tacks, and matches. The solution (tack the box to the wall as a shelf) was difficult because the box was seen only as a container, not as a possible shelf.

---

### 4. Prior Knowledge and Expertise

Prior knowledge facilitates problem solving by:
- Providing **relevant strategies and algorithms**
- Enabling **pattern recognition** in familiar problem types
- Building **schemas** that organise domain knowledge

However, expertise can also cause **fixation** — experts may be blind to unconventional solutions. **Novices** sometimes outperform experts on problems requiring fresh perspectives.

---

### 5. Individual Differences

| Factor | Effect |
|--------|--------|
| **Intelligence (especially Gf)** | Better performance on novel, complex problems |
| **Creativity** | More diverse solution generation, especially for ill-defined problems |
| **Working memory capacity** | Larger WM = better representation and tracking of solution paths |
| **Metacognition** | Self-monitoring improves strategy selection |

---

### 6. Motivation and Arousal

The **Yerkes-Dodson Law** predicts an **inverted-U relationship** between arousal and performance:
- Too little arousal → low effort, poor performance
- Optimal arousal → peak performance
- Too much arousal (stress/anxiety) → cognitive overload, poor performance

---

### 7. Incubation

**Setting aside a problem** temporarily can facilitate solution:
- Conscious attention is withdrawn, allowing unconscious processing
- Reduces fixation and mental set
- Returns to the problem with "fresh eyes"
- Wallas (1926) included incubation as a stage of creative problem solving

---

### 8. Environmental and Social Factors

- **Group settings** can facilitate (brainstorming) or hinder (groupthink) problem solving
- **Distractions** reduce available cognitive resources
- **Time pressure** can trigger simple heuristics but may reduce quality

---

### Conclusion

Problem solving performance is shaped by a rich interplay of cognitive, personality, motivational, and environmental factors. Key barriers include mental set, functional fixedness, and poor problem representation. Key facilitators include relevant prior knowledge, adequate working memory, optimal motivation, and well-timed incubation. Understanding these factors enables both individuals and organisations to structure conditions for more effective problem solving.

- Distinguish this topic from "Blocks to Problem Solving" — blocks are about external/emotional barriers; factors include cognitive facilitators too
