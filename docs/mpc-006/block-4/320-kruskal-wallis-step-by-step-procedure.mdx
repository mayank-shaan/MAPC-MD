# Kruskal-Wallis H Test: Step-by-Step Procedure

## The H Statistic: Formula and Logic

The Kruskal-Wallis test statistic **H** measures the degree to which rank sums differ across groups relative to what would be expected by chance:

$$H = \frac{12}{N(N+1)} \left[ \sum_{i=1}^{k} \frac{(\Sigma R_i)^2}{n_i} \right] - 3(N+1)$$

Where:
- $N$ = total number of observations across all groups
- $n_i$ = number of observations in group $i$
- $\Sigma R_i$ = sum of ranks in group $i$
- $k$ = number of groups

**Intuition**: If all groups were drawn from the same population, their rank sums should be roughly proportional to their sample sizes. When rank sums diverge substantially from this expectation, H becomes large — indicating real group differences.

---

## 3.5 Step-by-Step Procedure

### The Five Steps

**STEP 1**: Rank **all observations together** across all groups combined, from lowest (rank 1) to highest. For **tied scores**, assign the average of the ranks those values would have occupied.

**STEP 2**: Calculate the rank sum for each group: $T_1, T_2, ..., T_k$.

**STEP 3**: Compute the H statistic using the formula above.

**STEP 4**: Find the critical value (Table H for small samples; chi-square table for large samples).

**STEP 5**: Make a decision: **reject H₀ if H ≥ critical value**; otherwise accept H₀.

:::tip Direction of the Test
Unlike the Mann-Whitney U test (where smaller observed values indicate significance), the Kruskal-Wallis test is in the **same direction as ANOVA**: **larger H = more evidence against H₀**. Reject H₀ when H **exceeds** the critical value.
:::

## Worked Example 1: Noise Conditions and Task Errors (Small Sample)

### Research Question

Twelve participants were randomly assigned to three noise conditions (n = 4 each). Errors on a test were recorded. Does noise level affect performance?

| Condition A (Low Noise) | Condition B (Average Noise) | Condition C (Loud Noise) |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | 2 | 10 |
| 5 | 7 | 8 |
| 6 | 9 | 7 |
| 3 | 8 | 11 |

### Step 1: Rank All 12 Scores Together

| Score | Rank | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | 1 | Condition B |
| 3 | 2.5 | Conditions A, A (tied — positions 2 & 3, mean = 2.5) |
| 3 | 2.5 | |
| 5 | 4 | Condition A |
| 6 | 5 | Condition A |
| 7 | 6.5 | Conditions B, C (tied — positions 6 & 7, mean = 6.5) |
| 7 | 6.5 | |
| 8 | 8.5 | Conditions B, C (tied — positions 8 & 9, mean = 8.5) |
| 8 | 8.5 | |
| 9 | 10 | Condition B |
| 10 | 11 | Condition C |
| 11 | 12 | Condition C |

### Step 2: Rank Sums Per Group

| Condition A | Rank | Condition B | Rank | Condition C | Rank |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | 2.5 | 2 | 1 | 10 | 11 |
| 5 | 4 | 7 | 6.5 | 8 | 8.5 |
| 6 | 5 | 9 | 10 | 7 | 6.5 |
| 3 | 2.5 | 8 | 8.5 | 11 | 12 |
| **ΣT₁ = 14** | | **ΣT₂ = 26** | | **ΣT₃ = 38** | |

### Step 3: Compute H

$$H = \frac{12}{12(13)} \left[ \frac{14^2}{4} + \frac{26^2}{4} + \frac{38^2}{4} \right] - 3(13)$$

$$H = \frac{12}{156} \left[ 49 + 169 + 361 \right] - 39$$

$$H = 0.0769 \times 579 - 39 = 44.54 - 39 = \mathbf{5.54}$$

### Step 4 & 5: Decision

From **Table H** (k = 3, n = 4, 4, 4), the critical value at α = .05 is **5.692**.

Since H = 5.54 < 5.692, we **fail to reject H₀**.

**Conclusion**: There is no statistically significant difference in error rates across the three noise conditions (Low, Average, Loud).

## 3.6 Large Sample Considerations: Chi-Square Approximation

**When to use the chi-square table instead of Table H:**

Table H (exact Kruskal-Wallis table) has limited coverage:
- Up to 8 samples when k = 3
- Up to 4 samples when k = 4
- Up to 3 samples when k = 5

As sample sizes and group numbers increase beyond these limits, the **H statistic approximates a chi-square distribution** with degrees of freedom $df = k - 1$.

$$H \sim \chi^2_{(k-1)} \quad \text{for large samples}$$

:::note Power Consideration
For small samples, using the chi-square approximation slightly **reduces test power** (increases likelihood of retaining a false H₀). When exact tables are available, always prefer them.
:::

## Worked Example 2: Court Coordinator Training Needs (Large Sample)

### Research Question

A court administrator asked 24 court coordinators across three counties to rate their training needs (Likert scale 1–7, where 7 = critical need). Do the three counties differ in training need?

**Data**:

| County A (n = 8) | County B (n = 10) | County C (n = 6) |
|---|---|---|
| 3, 1, 3, 1, 5, 4, 4, 2 | 7, 6, 5, 7, 3, 1, 6, 4, 4, 5 | 4, 2, 5, 1, 6, 7 |

### Step 1: Rank All 24 Scores (Handling Ties)

Scores of 1 occupy positions 1, 2, 3, 4 → mean rank = **2.5**  
Scores of 2 occupy positions 5, 6 → mean rank = **5.5**  
Scores of 3 occupy positions 7, 8, 9 → mean rank = **8**  
Scores of 4 occupy positions 10, 11, 12, 13 → mean rank = **12**  
Scores of 5 occupy positions 14, 15, 16, 17 → mean rank = **16.5**  
Scores of 6 occupy positions 18, 19, 20 → mean rank = **20**  
Scores of 7 occupy positions 21, 22, 23 → mean rank = **23** *(Note: only 3 in this data set)*

### Step 2: Group Rank Sums

| | County A | County B | County C |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rank Sum (ΣR) | 67.0 | 153.5 | 79.5 |
| (ΣR)² | 4,489 | 23,562.25 | 6,320.25 |
| n | 8 | 10 | 6 |
| (ΣR)²/n | 561.125 | 2,356.225 | 1,053.375 |
| **Sum** | | **3,970.725** | |

### Step 3: Compute H

$$H = \frac{12}{24(25)} \times 3970.725 - 3(25)$$

$$H = 0.02 \times 3970.725 - 75 = 79.415 - 75 = \mathbf{4.42}$$

### Step 4 & 5: Chi-Square Decision

$df = k - 1 = 3 - 1 = 2$

Critical chi-square value at α = .05, df = 2: **χ² = 5.991**

Since H = 4.42 < 5.991, we **fail to reject H₀**.

**Conclusion**: There is no significant difference in training needs among court coordinators across the three counties.

## Kruskal-Wallis in SPSS

```
Analyse → Non-Parametric Tests → Legacy Dialogs → k Independent Samples
```

### Steps

1. **Analyse** → **Non-Parametric Tests** → **k Independent Samples**
2. Move the **test variable** (dependent variable, e.g., training need rating) to *Test Variable List*
3. Move the **grouping variable** (e.g., county) to *Grouping Variable*
4. Click **Define Range** → enter minimum and maximum group codes → **Continue**
5. Under **Test Type**, ensure **Kruskal-Wallis H** is checked
6. Click **OK**

### Reading SPSS Output

- **Mean Rank table**: Shows the average rank for each group — higher mean ranks indicate higher values
- **Test Statistics table**: Reports H statistic, df, and asymptotic significance (p-value)
- If p < α (e.g., < .05): reject H₀ — at least two groups differ significantly

## 🧠 Memory Aid

**The H Formula: "Twelve by N-total, sum squared-rank-sums over n, subtract three-N"**

$$H = \underbrace{\frac{12}{N(N+1)}}_{\text{scaling factor}} \underbrace{\sum \frac{T_i^2}{n_i}}_{\text{weighted rank sums}} - \underbrace{3(N+1)}_{\text{correction term}}$$

**Quick checks**:
- Always verify: $T_1 + T_2 + ... + T_k = N(N+1)/2$ (sum of all ranks = this value)
- Example: N = 12 → sum of ranks should = 12 × 13 / 2 = **78** (check: 14 + 26 + 38 = 78 ✓)

**Small vs. Large sample decision**:
- Small (within Table H limits) → use Table H critical value
- Large (beyond Table H) → use $\chi^2$ with df = k − 1
